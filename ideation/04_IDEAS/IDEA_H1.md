# IDEA_H1: Horizon laws and the restart identity — what ASR@k can and cannot tell you beyond the tested budget

Evidence grades used in this card: **[notes]** = identifier appears in the project notes; **[read]** = primary record read in this session (alphaXiv full text or publisher page); **[listing]** = only a search/index listing seen in this session; **[pre-check]** = my own small computation in this session (scripts in `04_IDEAS/H_prechecks/`), not independently reviewed.

- Gap addressed: G8 (budget-normalised comparison of red-teamers) as the primary gap; also G5 (adaptive evaluation of defences) and G3 (validity of ASR as a measure).

- Target venue and track: ICML 2027, topic area "Evaluation (methodology, meta-studies, replicability)"; contribution type **General** (theorems plus experiments). "Theory" type is possible only if Theorem 2 below is proved with matching lower bounds.

- Problem (1 paragraph): Agent-security papers report success within k attempts at budgets that cannot be compared (ASR@10 in 2609.33628, ASR@800 in 2610.41998, "up to 10 attempts" in 2608.30207, 150 and 320 queries in 2611.11861, all [notes]), and then rank attackers and defences across those budgets. For i.i.d. rollouts, a September 2026 preprint already shows that depth-n data identify only the first n moments of the latent per-task success distribution and gives sharp identified intervals for pass@k with k > n (2609.09245 [read]). Three things are still missing, and they are the ones a security evaluator needs: (a) a quantitative law for *how far* beyond the tested depth a k-attempt metric is pinned down, in the population and with N scenarios; (b) the effect of the red-teaming data format, where a run stops at the first success, as opposed to a fixed number of rollouts with a success count; (c) adaptive attackers, whose attempts are not exchangeable and which 2609.09245 (limitations section) and the trap model of 2609.32116 [read] explicitly exclude. Without (c), nobody can say whether a higher ASR@k comes from more attempts or from adaptation, which is the claim every adaptive-attack paper makes.

- Key insight (the non-obvious observation; 2–3 sentences): The worst-case ambiguity of failure-to-break at budget k given depth-n curves equals twice the best degree-n polynomial approximation error of x^k on [0,1], which is governed by n²/k and not by n/k: depth of order √k already pins the metric in the population, yet with N scenarios the usable polynomial degree is capped, so a real benchmark can extrapolate only by a factor of order log N, and stop-at-first-success logging shortens that horizon further. Second, heterogeneity across scenarios cancels in one specific comparison: for an attacker with no within-run memory, two independent restarts of depths j and l leave a scenario unbroken exactly as often as one run of depth j+l, so the difference between the two is a nonparametric measure of what adaptation adds, which the single-run curve can never supply.

- Approach (how the insight becomes a method/system/theory):
  - Model. Scenario i has a latent hazard sequence h_i(1), h_i(2), …; survival S_i(j) = Π_{t≤j}(1 − h_i(t)); the reported curve is S(j) = E[S_i(j)] = 1 − ASR@j. A memoryless attacker has h_i(t) = p_i for all t, so S(j) = E[(1−p)^j], a Hausdorff moment sequence.
  - Theorem 1 (population horizon; intended statement). Let W(n,k) be the supremum, over pairs of mixing laws with identical depth-n data distribution, of the difference in S(k). Then W(n,k) = 2·min over polynomials q of degree ≤ n of max_{x∈[0,1]} |x^k − q(x)|, and there are absolute constants c₁, c₂ with c₁·π(n,k) ≤ W(n,k) ≤ c₂·π(n,k), where π(n,k) is the probability that a fair ±1 walk of 2k steps ends beyond ±2n. Consequence: the tested depth must grow like √k, not like k; on the numerical law below, W ≤ δ corresponds to n ≈ √k · erfc⁻¹(δ), about 1.8·√k for δ = 0.01. Proof idea: LP duality for the truncated moment problem (the route 2609.09245 uses for sharp intervals), then the substitution x = y² and the monomial-approximation theorem of Newman and Rivlin (Aequationes Math. 14, 1976, pp. 451–455 [read: Springer abstract page]). [pre-check] A discretised minimax LP gives W = 0.67 for (n,k) = (10,800), 0.51 for (16,1000), 0.17 for (28,800), 0.164 whenever n² = k, 0.0043 for (64,1000); the values follow erfc(n/√k) within about 10% for n²/k ≤ 4. These are worst cases over count laws; a specific law can be much tighter, as 2609.09245 shows on GSM8K.
  - Theorem 2 (finite-N horizon; intended statement, lower bound not yet derived). For N scenarios, depth n and target budget k = t·n, the minimax root-mean-square error for S(k) is of the order of the χ²-modulus of continuity at 1/√N and is attained by an estimator of the form (1/N)Σ φ(data_i); for stop-at-first-success data it depends on (t, N) essentially alone and stays above ε unless t ≲ c·log N / log(1/ε); for the full-count design the usable degree is about √(n·log N), giving a longer horizon at the same N. Proof idea: the dualised Le Cam method of Polyanskiy and Wu (arXiv:1902.05616 [read]) for linear functionals of a mixing law, with the Bernstein-coefficient bound e^{d²/n} of Vinayak et al. (arXiv:1902.04553 [read]) for the count design; the log N factor mirrors the unseen-species limit of Orlitsky, Suresh and Wu (arXiv:1511.07428 [read]). [pre-check] Best linear estimator, worst-case bias plus ‖φ‖∞/√N: with N = 629 (the AgentDojo case count [notes]) and stop-at-first-success data, extrapolating by t = 2 costs 0.093, t = 3 costs 0.16, t = 10 costs 0.35, t = 80 costs 0.49, against 0.040 at t = 1; with N = 10⁷ the t = 80 figure is still 0.42. With full counts at N = 629 the same errors are 0.041 (t = 2), 0.083 (t = 3), 0.28 (t = 10).
  - Theorem 3 (adaptivity). (a) Observational equivalence: a depth-n survival curve is produced by some memoryless attacker over heterogeneous scenarios if and only if it extends to a completely monotone sequence; every non-increasing curve is produced by some history-dependent attacker on identical scenarios. So the curve can certify within-run dependence (a violation) but can never attribute a gain to adaptation. (b) Restart identity: with two independent runs per scenario, P(T₁ > j, T₂ > l) = S(j+l) for all j + l ≤ n under a memoryless attacker; the functional G(j,l) = E[S_i(j)S_i(l)] − S(j+l) is identified from two restarts with no model for heterogeneity, is positive when hazards rise within a run (learning) and negative when they fall (fatigue or mode collapse). (c) Two-world lower bound: for any completely monotone curve there is a memoryless-heterogeneous world and a fatiguing-homogeneous world with the same single-run data law and different G, so the best restart policy for a fixed query budget is not identified without restarts. Proof idea: (a) is the Hausdorff moment theorem; (b) is one line of conditional independence; (c) sets h(j) = 1 − S(j)/S(j−1). The single-spell versus multiple-spell contrast is the classical one in duration analysis (Elbers and Ridder 1982; Honoré 1993, both [listing: RePEc records]).
  - Deliverable for practitioners: a short reporting rule (tested depth n, scenario count N, design, the admissible claim horizon from Theorems 1–2, and G with an interval when adaptation is claimed) and a script that computes the bounds.
  - Boundary case most likely to break it: Theorem 2's lower bound for stop-at-first-success data (my numbers are upper bounds for linear estimators with a conservative variance proxy); and Theorem 3(b) if "independent restart" is not achievable because the target system or the attacker keeps state across runs (persistent memory, cached attacker models), which is exactly the memory-agent setting and must be controlled by environment resets.

- Contribution type: new theory/bound, with a new measurement protocol (the two-restart design).

- Claims (falsifiable, 2–4):
  1. W(n,k) is a function of n²/k up to constants and equals 2·E_n(x^k); refuted by an exact principal-representation computation that deviates from the LP values by more than a constant factor on a grid of (n,k) up to n = 64.
  2. On logged data with deep ground truth, no estimator fitted at depth n recovers S(t·n) better than the Theorem 2 curve for its N, and the parametric extrapolations in use (power law, Beta-binomial, log-normal trap model) disagree with each other by at least the identified-set width for t ≥ 3. Refuted if one parametric family stays within sampling error of the deep ground truth across all (defence, attacker) pairs.
  3. At equal N and t = 2, the full-count design has at most about half the worst-case extrapolation error of stop-at-first-success logging. Refuted by simulation or by re-cutting real logs.
  4. For at least one widely used history-dependent attacker on an agent benchmark, the single-run curve is statistically compatible with complete monotonicity while the two-restart statistic G is significantly non-zero; for a resampling attacker G is null. Refuted if G is null for all history-dependent attackers tested (then adaptation adds nothing beyond resampling at these budgets, which is itself a reportable negative result).

- Closest work known so far (with identifiers) and the expected delta:
  - 2609.09245 "What Fixed-Rollout pass@k Evaluations Can Identify" [read]: identification of exactly n moments, non-degenerate identified interval for every k > n, sharp intervals by Hausdorff principal representations, a coarse Jensen certificate. **This anticipates the identification core of this idea.** It gives no rate for the interval width, recommends depth ≥ k for direct claims, does not implement a finite-sample identified-set procedure, and excludes adaptive allocation and correlated completions. Delta: Theorems 1–3.
  - 2609.32116 trap model of Best-of-N jailbreaking [read]: a five-parameter log-normal mixture that extrapolates from N ≤ 100 to 10⁴ and beats a Beta mixture; restricted to i.i.d. augmentation kernels; reports a finite-size "resolution budget". Delta: distribution-free bounds that say when any such extrapolation is licensed.
  - 2601.18076 (NeurIPS 2025 position paper) [read]: Top-1-of-K changes the estimand; asks whether sophisticated attacks beat a resampling baseline. Delta: G answers that question for an attacker against its own restarts.
  - 2605.12869 [read]: Kaplan–Meier curves over 10 verbatim repetitions on 60 prompts; reads a decreasing pooled hazard as a property of the model. Delta: Theorem 3 shows that reading is not identified.
  - 2609.25173 [read]: reporting audit of 259 papers; minimum detectable differences under independence; 10.8% of papers state an attack budget. 2610.39124 [read]: clean-sheet certificates for milestone chains under a domination assumption on adaptive processes. Budget-aware ASR variants 2608.17360, 2606.11409, 2610.26589, 2610.25897 [listing, not read].
  - Strongest result a referee will compare against: Theorem 1 and the sharp intervals of 2609.09245 on the identification side, and the log n extrapolation limit of Orlitsky–Suresh–Wu together with the Polyanskiy–Wu duality on the minimax side.
  - Honest status: Theorem 1 is a corollary of moment-problem duality plus Newman–Rivlin; Theorem 2 is an instance of Polyanskiy–Wu with a modulus still to be computed; Theorem 3(a) is the Hausdorff theorem and 3(c) is the duration-analysis identification problem. What I did not find stated anywhere: the √k population law for k-attempt metrics, the stop-versus-count comparison, and the restart identity as a measure of adaptation.

- Evaluation sketch: settings, baselines (strongest first), metrics, decisive experiment
  - Settings. (i) Re-analysis at zero API cost of public deep-rollout data (the 10,000-rollout release analysed in 2609.09245; Best-of-N logs if released). (ii) New agent runs: AgentDojo/AutoDojo (2406.13352, 2606.15057 [notes]) and one memory benchmark (MemoryDojo 2611.09721 [notes]) with 2–3 defences, 2 mid-priced API models and 1 open model on the GPUs; attackers: a resampling attacker, a template library, and one history-dependent open attacker (PISmith 2603.13026 or AgentVigil 2505.05849 [notes]); depth 50–200, two restarts, about 100–300 scenarios.
  - Baselines, strongest first: sharp identified intervals of 2609.09245; trap-model and Beta-binomial extrapolation; power-law fit; binomial interval at the tested depth (current practice).
  - Metrics: coverage and width of bounds against deep ground truth; extrapolation error versus t and N; G(j,l) with scenario-clustered intervals; rank reversals between defences across budgets.
  - Decisive experiment: subsample the public deep-rollout data to depth n and N tasks, and check whether observed worst-case errors over many task subsets track the Theorem 2 curve; in parallel compute W(n,k) exactly. If real mixing laws are always far from the worst case (bounds never bind at N ≤ 1000), the paper loses practical force and should stop at a short note.

- Resources needed vs available (00_CONSTRAINTS.md): theory needs only a laptop (LPs of a few thousand variables). Agent runs: roughly 10⁴–10⁵ episodes; feasible with commercial APIs at small-model prices and one open model on 1–2 GPUs; frontier-model runs must be kept to a small confirmatory subset. Mathematical background: Chebyshev/Bernstein approximation, truncated moment problems and LP duality, Le Cam two-point bounds with χ² divergence, elementary survival analysis. No measure-theoretic machinery beyond that; the proofs of Theorems 1 and 3 are short, Theorem 2's lower bound is the hard part.

- Risks and unknowns:
  - Novelty: the base theorem exists (2609.09245); 2611.00474 and 2510.05197 [listing/cited, not read] may contain rates; a rate result could appear quickly from the same authors.
  - "Corollary" reading by an ICML theory referee; mitigated only by Theorem 2 with a matching lower bound and by the restart protocol on real agents.
  - Independence of restarts is hard to guarantee for stateful agents; judge noise (2609.25173) adds a floor that mimics easy scenarios.
  - Cost of deep ground truth on agent benchmarks limits the size of the confirmatory study.
