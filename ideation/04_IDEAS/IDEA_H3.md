# IDEA_H3: The storage budget is the collapse clock — write-back memory as a Pólya urn, budgeted consolidation as a Moran process

Evidence grades used in this card: **[notes]** = identifier appears in the project notes; **[read]** = primary record read in this session; **[listing]** = only a search/index listing seen in this session; **[pre-check]** = my own simulation in this session (`04_IDEAS/H_prechecks/h3_sim.py`), not independently reviewed.

- Gap addressed: G12 (homogenisation and knowledge collapse × agent memory, including Vietnamese person reference), with a measurement link to G1; secondary relevance to G5 (how long a benchmark must run to see persistence or decay).

- Target venue and track: ACL 2027 via the ARR January 2027 cycle, special theme "Homogenization and knowledge collapse in LLMs" (the call accepts theoretical papers [notes]); long paper. Fallback areas: "LLM Agents for NLP" or "Machine Learning for NLP".

- Problem (1 paragraph): The theme lists training data, alignment and decoding as mechanisms of homogenisation. Agent memory adds a fourth that operates at deployment time: the agent retrieves records, answers, and writes the answer or a consolidated summary back. Memory Echo (2610.17763 [notes; read]) measures the resulting proliferation of same-source records in four frameworks but has no model, and runs six write-backs and thirty read-only probes. Nothing predicts whether a minority variant survives, drifts or is erased as a function of memory policy; here "variant" can be a minority claim, or a language-specific form such as Vietnamese kinship-anchored person reference that a memory writer tends to rewrite into an English-style "user / I" frame. Nor does anything say how many sessions a benchmark needs before collapse is visible.

- Key insight (the non-obvious observation; 2–3 sentences): A memory store under write-back is a reinforcement process whose step size is set by the memory-management policy: append-only memory is a generalised Pólya urn with step 1/t, in which a writer's normalisation bias δ erodes the minority share only polynomially, as (t/n₀)^{−δ}, and without bias the share freezes at a random level; fixed-budget memory with overwrite or rewrite-all consolidation is a Moran-type chain with step 1/B, in which the minority form dies out almost surely at exponential rate δ/B and, even with δ = 0, pure drift fixes one variant. The budget that was introduced to save cost is therefore what turns a slow, almost invisible drift into certain monoculture, and the urn's drift function is the Bernstein polynomial of the reader's aggregation curve, so it can be measured in a handful of single-step probes.

- Approach (how the insight becomes a method/system/theory):
  - Model. Records are of type A (minority or language-specific variant) or B. Each session retrieves k records exchangeably; with j of type A the reader answers in form A with probability g(j/k); the writer turns an A answer into B with probability δ; the answer is written back. Let x_t be the share of A. The one-step drift is f(x) = (1−δ)·B_k[g](x), where B_k is the Bernstein operator of degree k.
  - Theorem 1 (append-only memory; intended statement). x_{t+1} = x_t + (ξ_{t+1} − x_t)/(n₀+t+1) with E[ξ | x_t] = f(x_t). (a) If g is the identity and δ = 0, x_t converges almost surely to a non-degenerate random limit and E[x_t] = x₀. (b) If g is the identity and δ > 0, E[x_t] = x₀·Π_{s≤t}(1 − δ/(n₀+s)) ≈ x₀·((n₀+t)/n₀)^{−δ}; extinction is certain in the limit but the half-life is about n₀·2^{1/δ} sessions. (c) For general g, x_t converges almost surely to a stable fixed point of f. Proof idea: (a) is the classical Pólya urn; (c) is the strong law for generalised urn processes of Hill, Lane and Sudderth (Ann. Probab. 8(2), 1980, pp. 214–226 [listing: Project Euclid and bibliography records; abstract page blocked]) and the stochastic-approximation treatment in Pemantle's survey (arXiv:math/0610076 [listing]).
  - Theorem 2 (fixed budget B with overwrite; intended statement). The share is a birth–death chain on {0, 1/B, …, 1}. (a) With g the identity and δ > 0, E[x_t] = x₀(1 − δ/B)^t and A is lost almost surely. (b) With δ = 0, x_t is a martingale, A fixes with probability x₀ and absorption takes order B² sessions. (c) With a majority-amplifying g, fixation is selection-driven: it is reached much sooner than under neutral drift, and a form that starts below the unstable fixed point wins less often than its initial share, increasingly so as B grows. Proof idea: Moran's process (Moran 1958, as cited in 2603.24676 [read]); the logistic fixation law is the one derived for populations of agents in 2603.24676, eq. (16).
  - Theorem 3 (structure). Because B_k fixes linear functions and is variation diminishing, the number of interior fixed points of f is at most the number of sign changes of (1−δ)·g(j/k) − j/k over the grid j = 0, …, k (for δ = 0, the number of times the reader's curve crosses the diagonal); with k = 1 the process is of Pólya type for every g. Consequence: lock-in requires retrieval depth k ≥ 3 together with a majority-amplifying reader; diversity is preserved by a reader whose curve crosses the diagonal from above, or by serving one representative per source family (the FPA gate of 2610.17763), which makes the drift independent of record counts. The variation-diminishing property is a textbook fact for which I have not yet fixed a primary citation (a search in this session returned arXiv:1609.00973 on a related statement [listing, not read]).
  - Theorem 4 (evaluation horizon). To observe a relative loss Δ of the minority form with fixed power, a benchmark needs about (B/δ)·ln(1/(1−Δ)) sessions under budgeted memory and about n₀·((1−Δ)^{−1/δ} − 1) sessions under append-only memory, before sampling noise is accounted for. With δ = 0.05, B = n₀ = 50 and Δ = 0.2 these are about 220 and 4,300 sessions.
  - [pre-check] Simulation with k = 5, n₀ = B = 50, x₀ = 0.4, 2,000 runs: with δ = 0.05, append-only gives mean share 0.308 after 10,000 sessions (formula 0.307, no extinctions), budgeted gives 0.140 after 1,000 sessions (formula 0.147) and extinction in every run by 10,000; with δ = 0, budgeted memory fixes A in 39% of runs (prediction 40%) while append-only shows no fixation; with a majority-amplifying reader, budgeted memory fixes within 1,000 sessions and the minority form wins 21% of runs. The Bernstein drift of that reader has exactly one interior crossing for k = 3, 5, 9 and none for k = 1.
  - Empirical instantiation. Measure g by single-step probes (k+1 conditions) and δ by annotating one write pass: two Vietnamese annotators label whether a kinship-anchored reference (which encodes relative age, gender and relation) survives the writer or is rewritten to a generic frame. Predict multi-session trajectories from (g, δ, k, B) and compare with real loops.
  - Boundary case most likely to break it: non-exchangeable retrieval. Deterministic top-k by similarity to a fixed query, or strong recency weighting, means that the same few records are always served; the state is then not summarised by x_t. The standard repair is an effective population size B_eff fitted from the drift variance; if no B_eff fits, the urn abstraction fails for that system and the claim must be restricted.

- Contribution type: new theory/bound (a separation between memory policies) with a new measurement protocol.

- Claims (falsifiable, 2–4):
  1. With reader, writer and δ held fixed, switching from append-only to fixed-budget overwrite changes the decay of the minority form from a power law with exponent δ to an exponential with rate δ/B. Refuted if both policies show the same law in real loops or if the rate does not scale as 1/B for B ∈ {20, 50}.
  2. Single-step measurements of g and δ predict multi-session trajectories within the simulation's 90% band for at least three memory frameworks and two models. Refuted if trajectories leave the band systematically (history effects beyond the share).
  3. A probability-matching reader shows no lock-in under append-only memory and a majority-amplifying reader shows bistable lock-in; family-level gating removes the drift. Refuted by lock-in with a matching reader or persistence under gating.
  4. Benchmarks with 5 to 30 rounds have less than 20% power to detect δ ≤ 0.05 under append-only memory; shown by computation and by one short-versus-long rerun.

- Closest work known so far (with identifiers) and the expected delta:

Delta: records as the population, the step-size dichotomy between append-only and budgeted memory, writer normalisation as mutation, the horizon result, a cross-lingual instantiation with human labels.
  - 2610.17763 Memory Echo [notes; read]: measurement and a provenance gate; no model, short horizon. Delta: a predictive law, and an explanation of why its gate works.

  - Budget-centred memory theory that treats the budget only as cost: 2605.10870 (DeMem), 2610.24955 (MemPrice) [notes].
  - Strongest result a referee will compare against: the drift–selection scaling laws of 2603.24676 and the strong law for generalised urns.

New content: the mapping, the policy separation with its rates, the horizon consequence, and the measurement protocol.

- Evaluation sketch: settings, baselines (strongest first), metrics, decisive experiment
  - Settings: (i) controlled variant pairs (two competing answers; standard versus kinship-anchored Vietnamese reference in English–Vietnamese dialogues written for the study, no real user data); (ii) memory frameworks A-MEM, Mem0 and LangMem in append-only mode and with a fixed budget B ∈ {20, 50}; (iii) two API models plus one open model on the GPUs; 200–300 sessions, 20–30 independent runs per cell.
  - Baselines, strongest first: the population model of 2603.24676 with parameters mapped naively (population = B, bandwidth = k); a memoryless decay fit; no-model extrapolation from the first 5 rounds.
  - Metrics: share trajectory and its variance across runs, extinction and fixation frequencies, fitted decay law (power versus exponential, by likelihood ratio), calibration of predicted bands, inter-annotator agreement on the normalisation label.
  - Decisive experiment: one framework, one model, B = 20 against append-only, 300 sessions, 30 runs. If the two decay laws cannot be told apart, or the budgeted rate is not close to δ/B, the central claim is false.
