# IDEA_V1 (merged): Vietnamese person reference as a minimal-pair probe of instruction authority in indirect prompt injection

Merged by the coordinator from three independently generated cards that converged on the same idea. The full cards are the definition of the idea; read all three:
- IDEA_S2.md (security perspective) — "Borrowed voice: person-reference congruence as an implicit authority cue"
- IDEA_M3.md (measurement perspective) — "Who is speaking? Vietnamese address terms as a minimal-pair probe of instruction provenance"
- IDEA_L3.md (linguistics perspective) — "Who is being told what? Force and addressee underdetermination in Vietnamese"

- Gap addressed: G2 (touches G12)
- Target venue and track: ACL 2027 via ARR — "LLM Agents for NLP" or "Multilingualism and Cross-Lingual NLP"
- Merged core: Vietnamese self/addressee terms (kinship terms used as pronouns, e.g. anh–em, quý khách) obligatorily encode who speaks to whom. An instruction planted in tool/web/app content must therefore either match or mismatch the established user–assistant dyad. This yields minimal pairs (same content, position, language; only the address dyad or the force marking changes) that test whether agents assign authority by linguistic identity. English "I/you" and machine-translated benchmarks cannot express the contrast. A second factor (from L3): Vietnamese bare verb phrases are ambiguous between log line, plan and command, so the VI–EN gap should concentrate on particle-less items.
- Contribution type: new measurement/benchmark (+ mechanism finding)
- Generator perspective: merged (security + measurement + linguistics)
