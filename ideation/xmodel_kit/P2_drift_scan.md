## Closest works for the NEW elements (identifier — what it does — delta — does the delta matter to a referee?)

### Element 1 — prevalence of wrong PASS on real agent runs

1. **MobileJudgeBench, arXiv:2608.11434 [full].** 931 human-annotated trajectories from six mobile benchmarks, four agents; the AndroidWorld part is 205 trajectories over 52 tasks. The inset table of its Figure 3 scores AndroidWorld's built-in rule-based checker against the human labels on that subset: accuracy 94.6, **precision 98.6**, recall 87.2. Read as verdict errors, that is roughly 1.4% of native PASS verdicts and roughly 13% of human-judged successes rejected. The earlier hunters logged this paper at abstract level as "LLM-judge reliability"; the checker-vs-human numbers are only in the body. — *Delta:* a by-product, not a claim (the text calls rule-based checkers "reliable by construction"); labels come from screenshots, actions and UI trees, so collateral state damage is invisible to the annotators; 52 of 116 templates; no interval, no count of passes (1.4% is plausibly a single trajectory); pairwise annotator agreement 88.4%. — *Matters:* yes, strongly. The card's sentence "for mobile benchmarks nothing is known" is not correct as written, and this figure sits between C1's two pre-specified readings (< 1% and > 3%). It is the prior a referee will hold: wrong passes on AndroidWorld are rare, wrong fails are roughly ten times more common.

2. **AgentRewardBench, arXiv:2504.08942 [full].** 1,302 expert-annotated trajectories, four agents, five web benchmarks; annotators also mark side effects. Table 1 scores the official rule-based evaluators against expert labels: precision 83.8 overall (WebArena 79.0, VisualWebArena 85.2, WorkArena 100.0, WorkArena++ 83.3, AssistantBench 25.0), recall 55.9. Table 3 shows a rank change: rule-based scoring puts Qwen2.5-VL above GPT-4o on WebArena and WorkArena++, expert labels reverse it. — *Delta:* web only; trajectory review, no retained state; the paper's message is under-reporting, so the 16% wrong-PASS share is never discussed; single annotator per trajectory (89.3% agreement on one subset); no intervals; no predictor. — *Matters:* yes. "Prevalence is unmeasured outside coding benchmarks" is false as worded: both error directions and an ordering change on real runs were published for web benchmarks in April 2025. Neither idea card nor either earlier report lists it for this purpose.

3. **Other audits on real runs, all one direction or another domain.** arXiv:2610.01491 [abs] (WebArena-Lite, 165 tasks, six conditions): human review recovers 5.45–8.49 points of evaluator false negatives and *retains the original score otherwise* — FAIL direction only. WebArena Verified, OpenReview 94tlGxmqkN [abs]: audit of all 812 tasks, reports about 11% fewer false negatives on a baseline agent; no wrong-PASS rate. OSWorld-Verified blog [page]: 300+ feedback items fixed, mostly evaluators; the examples given are valid alternatives wrongly rejected; no measured error rate on agent runs, no audit of passing runs, no before/after ordering. arXiv:2507.02825 ABC [abs]: design flaws that inflate or deflate scores by up to 100% relative (e.g. τ-bench crediting empty responses). arXiv:2610.07274 [abs]: 22.6% of 84 recorded passes on Agents' Last Exam clear four post-hoc checks. OpenReview zh33pqlBjf [abs]: see element 3. — *Matters:* they bound what "first" can claim: F1S can be first for wrong PASS *with state-level evidence and intervals on mobile*, not first to audit GUI-agent verdicts against humans.

Not found: any "AndroidWorld-Verified" style re-release, any human re-audit of passes for AndroidLab, MobileWorld, Windows Agent Arena or macOSWorld, any correction of a mobile leaderboard.

### Element 2 — whole-state differential oracle

4. **OpenReview kuSAqpyELw, "Do Agents Do What Users Ask? Measuring Intent-State Consistency in Tool-Using Language Agents", ICLR 2027 submission [abs].** From one initial state it replays a reference execution and the agent's execution, ties tool calls to state transitions, and reports missing required effects, persistent unauthorised effects and transient ones; 1,600 AgentDojo trajectories, a human-reviewed sample of 300 (F1 0.811 full-trajectory vs 0.788 final-state-only). — *Delta:* tool-API agents, security framing, a supplied intent contract; no GUI or mobile state; no handling of several correct routes or nondeterministic fields; not compared with a native checker. — *Matters:* yes for C4. "Diff the agent's end state against a reference run from the same start, validate on a human sample" is under review at the venue family F1S targets.

5. **Native designs F1S would be compared with.** τ-bench, arXiv:2406.12045 [abs]: the benchmark's own oracle compares the end-of-conversation database with an annotated goal state. AppWorld, arXiv:2407.18901 [abs]: state-based unit tests that admit different solution routes and check for unexpected changes ("collateral damage"). — *Matters:* yes. Gold-end-state comparison is a 2024 design, so the oracle is new only for GUI/mobile state and for the measured mask; and on AppWorld (the optional third benchmark) the instrument largely duplicates the native checker — arXiv:2610.09142 already probes the residue.

6. **Diffy, Twitter engineering blog, 3 Sep 2015 [page].** Regression testing by response diff with "noise cancellation": two instances of known-good code are compared with each other to measure nondeterministic differences, and those are discounted from the candidate-vs-primary diff. — *Delta:* service responses, same-code replicas, no notion of distinct correct routes. — *Matters:* at ISSTA/ICSE (the step-down venues) a referee will name it; "mask learned from repeated correct runs" needs the route-diversity part to be new.

7. **Smaller neighbours.** OpenReview i7FGehiANA [abs] (ACL ARR Oct 2026): end-state verifiers credit goal states left by earlier tasks; a provenance-guarded twin verifier measures 6.9–19.6 points of inflation on pre-satisfiable tasks in a synthetic persistent testbed; 9 of 16 audited benchmark settings could credit residue — the pre-satisfied-goal route to a wrong PASS, relevant to the 46 postcondition-only AndroidWorld checkers. android_world commit c8fb7fc / PR #325 [git] (2025-08-30): logs all phone settings before and after an action through ADB "for tracking the presence of unsafe or unintended actions" — an upstream partial state observer (settings only, logging only). arXiv:2608.14131 LegacyWorld (already known). State-reading LLM verifiers that C4 must beat: arXiv:2610.40578 VeriCUA [abs] (desktop, mobile, web), OpenReview pezqXJvBms IRA [abs], arXiv:2611.02624 [abs] (state-only vs trajectory judge for harm; a combined judge was worse than the state-only one).

### Element 3 — do audit scores predict wrong passes?

8. **OpenReview zh33pqlBjf, "Red-Teaming the Grader: Mutation-Validated Constraint Checking for Tool-Agent Benchmarks", NeurIPS 2026 Verify-Agents workshop [abs].** Mutates stored reference trajectories of an 800-scenario video-editing benchmark in five ways (527 surviving candidate sites), repairs the final-state checker, then re-executes old and new scorers on the *stored final states of real agent runs*: 412 passes become failures and 482 failures become passes. — *Delta:* one domain; verdict changes are counted, not audited by humans (the abstract says so); no per-task relation between mutant survival and wrong passes. — *Matters:* yes. It is the one record found that carries a mutation audit through to verdict changes on real runs, and it shows the audit moved verdicts in both directions. It is absent from the card.

9. **Coding-side coupling evidence (the question is not untouched for LLM outputs).** arXiv:2607.22880 [abs]: replication of the coverage/mutation-vs-real-bug-detection studies on LLM-generated test suites; the correlation is context dependent and fails when the code under test may be buggy. arXiv:2609.09315 [abs]: on 6,000+ faulty LLM-generated programs, mutation testing is only marginally better than coverage at triggering and detecting the faults. arXiv:2606.16062 [abs]: across 134 SWE-bench Verified submissions, Pass@1 is 14.14 points higher on tasks whose tests admit a verified incorrect patch — verifier weakness correlated with observed pass rates on real submissions. OpenReview M6XpzcJAjQ [abs] (NeurIPS 2026 TAE): formal result that a passed evaluator audit does not identify held-out evaluator behaviour. — *Delta:* test suites and coding tasks, or theory; none relates per-task kill rate of a state checker to the audited wrong-pass rate of agents. — *Matters:* they must be cited next to Just et al. 2014; C2 stays open for agent graders.

Not found: any correlation of per-task or per-template verifier mutation score with audited wrong passes of agents; any hardening study outside coding that reports before/after orderings (arXiv:2610.15924 [abs] reports attack-success reduction on Terminal Bench, KernelBench and SETA, not leaderboard change).

### Element 4 — ranking inversion under differential grader error

10. **arXiv:2609.25173 [full].** Section V-B gives the inversion condition for an oracle with a *shared* false-positive rate α and *system-specific* false-negative rates β (their Eq. 2), calls it "a demanding condition" (1 of 25 parameter combinations inverts at a 10-point true gap) and states in the limitations that no defences were run and no empirical inversion is reported. — *Matters:* the card's description is accurate on "no measurement". Two details for C3: the published condition covers differential false negatives; differential wrong PASS (differing α) needs the general form, and the paper's own numbers predict that inversions need large differential error — so a null on C3 is the expected outcome.

11. **Empirical re-grading with ordering changes outside coding.** OpenReview 8PIpkkfZ84 [abs] (already known to the hunters; ICLR 2027 submission): repairing side-effect omissions flips rankings on four suites including τ²-bench banking, and about a third of flips pick the worse model on held-out outputs; a "gap > 2 standard errors" rule removes the adverse flips; matched random pass removal is the control. arXiv:2611.04399 [abs] (3 Nov 2026): on HAL and τ²-bench, grading the ungraded half of one agent's episodes closes a 13-point gap and an announced evaluator fix reorders a leaderboard. arXiv:2611.13541 [abs] (10 Nov 2026): correcting SWE-bench Verified test errors reorders neighbouring agents no more than deleting a random sixth of each agent's passes. AgentRewardBench Table 3 (item 2). — *Delta:* none is mobile or GUI-state based; none fits a per-agent wrong-pass model. — *Matters:* yes. C3 is a replication on a new benchmark family, and referees will expect the random-pass-removal control and a held-out check.

## Anything that anticipates a claim (C1–C4) or the key insight

- **Key insight (blind spot AND failure must coincide; existence need not predict prevalence).** Not found stated for agent graders. Partial support already in print: arXiv:2610.09142 says its counts give no prevalence estimate (earlier report); M6XpzcJAjQ proves the audit-pass identification gap in the abstract; MobileJudgeBench's 98.6% precision on AndroidWorld, set beside the code reading "46 of 116 checkers test only a postcondition", is an unremarked instance of weak-looking checkers with few wrong passes. Not anticipated, but the card under-cites the evidence.
- **C1 (prevalence, both directions).** Partly anticipated. Web: AgentRewardBench gives both directions on 1,302 real trajectories. Mobile: MobileJudgeBench gives both directions for AndroidWorld's checker on 205 trajectories; arXiv:2605.10448 gives bounds (earlier report). What remains new: all 116 templates, a second mobile benchmark, state-level evidence (so unrequested writes count), template-clustered intervals. Two sentences of the card need rewriting: "unmeasured outside coding benchmarks" and "for mobile benchmarks nothing is known".
- **C2 (audit scores do not predict prevalence).** Not anticipated. Nearest: zh33pqlBjf (audit-driven repair changes verdicts on real runs, uncorrelated), arXiv:2606.16062 (weak-test flag correlates with pass rate in coding), arXiv:2607.22880 and 2609.09315 (mutation score vs real faults for LLM-generated code). This is the claim that carries the novelty.
- **C3 (differential wrong-pass rates; ordering changes).** Anticipated in kind outside coding by 8PIpkkfZ84, arXiv:2611.04399 and AgentRewardBench Table 3; the analytic condition in 2609.25173 covers differential false negatives. New only as a mobile measurement with a mixed model. The phrase "the empirical test of the inversion condition" overstates it.
- **C4 (oracle cost and precision).** The oracle is a recombination: goal-state comparison (τ-bench), collateral-damage checks (AppWorld), reference-vs-agent replay with human validation (kuSAqpyELw), noise cancellation from repeated known-good runs (Diffy). New: route-diverse gold with a measured false-alarm rate on Android app databases, content providers and settings. The LLM state-reading baseline now has strong published forms (VeriCUA, IRA), which C4 has to beat rather than a home-made prompt.

## Verdict for the redesigned idea: **incremental**

Reason: no record anticipates the core pairing — a preregistered test of whether verifier-audit scores predict audited wrong passes of agents (C2), carried out on mobile benchmarks with a state-level oracle. C2 is clean in every source searched. Everything around it has moved closer than the card says: both-direction verdict error on real runs is published for web (AgentRewardBench) and, in small, for AndroidWorld itself (MobileJudgeBench: about 1.4% of native passes, about 13% of successes rejected); gold-end-state comparison with collateral checks is a 2024 benchmark design and has a 2026 replay-based sibling under review; ordering changes after grader repair are shown outside coding by three records, two of them from November 2026. The paper stands or falls on C2 and on the state-level evidence that screenshots cannot give; C1 alone would read as a larger replication, and the most likely C1 outcome is the "wrong passes are rare, wrong fails are not" result that MobileJudgeBench already hints at.

Corrections the card needs before Stage 8: (i) cite AgentRewardBench and MobileJudgeBench as prior prevalence evidence and drop the two "nothing is known / unmeasured" sentences; (ii) add zh33pqlBjf, kuSAqpyELw, i7FGehiANA, τ-bench and AppWorld native oracles, Diffy, arXiv:2611.04399, 2611.13541, 2607.22880, 2609.09315, 2606.16062; (iii) restate C3 as a replication with the random-pass-removal control; (iv) pin benchmark commits (MobileWorld fixed a wrong-PASS checker on 2026-07-02); (v) since wrong FAIL looks about ten times more frequent than wrong PASS on AndroidWorld, decide whether the headline is still wrong passes.

Not searched / limits: GitHub issue bodies and the whole MobileWorld tracker (proxy 403; android_world titles came from a third-party mirror and may lag); AndroidLab, OSWorld, WebArena and Windows Agent Arena trackers; Hugging Face discussion threads on OSWorld v2 evaluators (titles seen in a search result only); citation graphs of 2609.25173, 2610.09142 and 2605.10448 (Semantic Scholar 429); OpenReview PDFs and venue-wide enumeration (all OpenReview items are abstract-level); Google Scholar, ACL Anthology, IEEE/ACM libraries; non-English sources; vendor blogs and social posts by leaderboard maintainers. MobileJudgeBench's per-benchmark counts for the rule-based checker were read from one figure inset; the number of native passes behind the 98.6% was not found. A search that returned nothing is not proof of absence.
