- Closest works (3–5, more if needed): identifier — what it does — delta — does the delta matter to a referee?
  1. **arXiv:2603.12277, "Prompt Injection as Role Confusion" (Ye, Cui, Hadfield-Menell; ICML 2026, PMLR 306) [full].** Mechanistic account: models infer who is speaking from how text sounds, not from the role tag. Role probes show user-style text wrapped in tool tags is still represented as user (Userness 76–88%, Toolness under 20%, four open models); removing the style of a forged reasoning trace drops attack success from 61% to 10%; probe-measured confusion predicts agent exfiltration success (2% to 70% across quantiles). — *Delta:* English only; "style" is a bundle changed by LLM rewriting, not a single grammatical feature; no cue specific to *this* user (generic user-likeness, not the user's own dyad); no human attribution baseline; no multilingual or translation-pivot test. — *Matters?* Partly. This paper already owns the claim "agents assign authority by linguistic identity rather than channel". S2's sentence that injection research "has located authority in position" and that the question is "unknown" is no longer true, and none of the three cards cites this paper. A referee who knows it will read the Vietnamese dyad as one more style cue unless the paper shows what the minimal pair adds (a single controlled feature, a user-specific rather than role-generic cue, a human ceiling).
  2. **arXiv:2610.40396, "Understanding and Mitigating Role Spoofing in LLMs" (Edelman, Edelman, Goel, Malach; Apple/Penn/Princeton) [full].** Names the attack in which tool text impersonates the user; separates content cues (semantics, writing style) from structure cues (fake role tokens); a "Who Said What" explicit-attribution probe (the model is asked who sent a message) with misleading-content vs fake-structure conditions; over 95% impersonation on frontier models when both are combined; linear-probe detector. — *Delta:* English; content manipulation is whole-message swapping, not a minimal pair; no human baseline; no language factor. — *Matters?* Yes for M3: read-out (b) "explicit attribution in a separate call" and the recognition/execution framing are close to this paper's Who-Said-What setting, so that part is a replication in a new language, not a new instrument. The human-vs-model gap (M3 C2) is still new.
  3. **arXiv:2607.23545, XIH-Bench, "Language Shapes Instruction Hierarchy Compliance in Multilingual LLMs" (Moon, Hwang, Jung; EMNLP 2026 main) [full].** 78,894 instances, six languages (EN, DE, ES, FR, HI, ZH), three hierarchy settings including User > Tool, all 36 ordered language pairs. Cross-language conflicts are resolved in favour of the higher-priority source more often than same-language ones (User > Tool: same 55.72 vs cross 61.96 HCR), the "Language Boundary Effect"; positive for all 15 language pairs. — *Delta:* single-turn, not an executable agent; items made by an LLM translation pipeline; no Vietnamese; language is a label, no within-language grammatical factor. — *Matters?* The language-congruence claims (S2 C1, M3 C4, L3 C4) are anticipated in direction and in the user-vs-tool setting. L3 says so and frames C4 as replication; S2 presents C1 as a primary claim without citing XIH-Bench. A referee will treat congruence as known and ask only whether it carries to agents. The dyad and force factors are untouched by this paper.
  4. **arXiv:2406.13352, AgentDojo (NeurIPS 2024 D&B), Table 2 [full].** Ablation of attacker knowledge in the "important message" injection, which claims to come from the user: generic "the user" 45.8% targeted ASR; correct user name 46.1%; *wrong* user name 23.2%; wrong model name 23.7%. — *Delta:* English, an explicit name inside an explicit authorship claim, one model (GPT-4o). — *Matters?* Yes. It is existing evidence for the asymmetry the idea predicts (a matching identity cue adds little; a mismatching one roughly halves compliance), and it is exactly the "English identity control" that S2 lists as a new baseline. It also undercuts the wording "English cannot express the contrast": English expresses it lexically; what Vietnamese adds is that the cue is obligatory and implicit.
  5. **DOI 10.33778/kcsa.2025.25.5.117, Lee and Chang, "Multilingual Prompt-Injection-Based Jailbreak Vulnerability Analysis: A Comparative Study of English, Korean Plain Speech, and Honorific Style", Journal of Information and Security (융합보안논문지, KCI) 25(5):117–129, Dec 2025 [abs: Crossref record + publisher abstract page; full text paywalled].** Compares attack success for English, plain-style Korean and honorific-style Korean on general and Korean-specialised models with role-play, parameter-manipulation and GCG-type attacks; averages are close (about 1.5% vs 2.0%), with double-digit rates only for specific model-language pairs; the authors propose honorific style as a safety-bypass route. — *Delta:* direct jailbreak in chat, not indirect injection; honorific *level*, not speaker-addressee identity or congruence with the user; tiny effects. — *Matters?* It falsifies the cards' statements that no work ties honorifics to injection or safety (S2: "found none on authority or injection"; M3 search note). It must be cited; it does not anticipate the dyad-congruence design.
  6. **ACL Anthology 2026.wmt-main.16, Huang and Tsai, "Evaluating Pragmatic Appropriateness in English–Vietnamese Machine Translation with Dyadic Profiles" (WMT 2026) [abs + bib].** Sender–recipient profiles for EN→VI translation of 30 workplace requests, two native judges, role-reversal test (20/20 Gemini translations appropriate under the intended relationship, 4 after reversal). — *Delta:* translation quality, no security, no agents. — *Matters?* It is the nearest computational treatment of Vietnamese dyads with LLMs and of the role-reversal minimal-pair idea. It also qualifies the cards' claim that MT "neutralises or drops the pair": current LLM translation chooses a plausible dyad even without a profile, so the machine-translated baseline may not be dyad-free; it will carry an arbitrary dyad, which is a different confound.
  7. **arXiv:2607.01153, "Adversarial Pragmatics for AI Safety Evaluation" (Reynolds) [full]** and **arXiv:2603.25015, "Imperative Interference" [abs; L3 read it in full].** The first defines an evaluation family over embedded commands, mention/use and quotation, deixis, indirect speech acts and speech-act force with paired contrasts, including a tool-result pair; 18 English seed items, one adjudicator; it states that deixis, politeness and source attribution vary across languages and each language needs its own reference judgments. The second shows mood/register (imperative vs declarative) changes cross-lingual instruction adherence for trusted prompts. — *Delta:* tiny, English or MT, no injection benchmark, no Vietnamese, no human agreement study. — *Matters?* They anticipate L3's framing (force and addressee as pragmatic variables; quoted/reported instructions as look-alikes) but not its predictions or data.

- Parts of the idea already anticipated (be specific, claim by claim):
  - *Core mechanism "authority is assigned by linguistic identity, not channel"* (S2 key insight, M3 C1 in general form): anticipated in English by 2603.12277 and 2610.40396; social-authority cues beating role tags by 2502.15851.
  - *Matched identity adds little, mismatched identity lowers compliance* (S2 C2 ordering; S2 baseline 4 "English identity control"; M3 baseline 1): anticipated for explicit names by AgentDojo Table 2.
  - *Language congruence between user and injected text* (S2 C1, M3 C4, L3 C4): anticipated in a non-agent User > Tool setting by XIH-Bench (Language Boundary Effect); independent variation of request/page/response language by 2610.23093; planned for agents by BabelDojo (unpublished).
  - *Explicit "who wrote this" attribution probe and recognition-vs-execution gap* (M3 read-out b, part of C2): anticipated by the Who-Said-What setting of 2610.40396 and the role probes of 2603.12277.
  - *English-trained detectors degrade on non-English injections* (second half of S2 C3; part of L3 C2): anticipated by 2610.22244, LLMail-Inject (multilingual text used to evade defences), MIPIAD, Lakera's report [blog, fetched]. Benign look-alikes and detector false positives as a metric (L3 approach 2): anticipated in English by the works L3 already cites and by AlignSentinel 2602.13597 and PIDS-Bench 2609.15017 [abs].
  - *Honorific/register as a safety variable*: anticipated at chat level for Korean (KCI paper) and for detector evasion in JA/KO (2306.09752).
  - *Mood/force and deixis as pragmatic variables in instruction following* (L3 framing): partly anticipated by 2603.25015 and 2607.01153.
  - *Dyad-sensitive evaluation of Vietnamese with role reversal*: anticipated for MT by WMT 2026 (Huang and Tsai).

- Parts with no prior work found:
  - Vietnamese (or any open-class person-reference system) self/addressee terms manipulated as a **minimal pair inside an injected instruction**, congruent vs incongruent with the user's established dyad, with content, position and language fixed. No paper, dataset card or repository found does this for Vietnamese, Thai, Japanese, Korean or Indonesian.
  - A **native-speaker authorship-attribution baseline** on the same sentences, and the human-vs-model gap (M3 C2).
  - **Translate-to-English pivot as an eraser of a provenance cue** (S2 C4, M3 C3). Related pivot and translation-bypass work exists, but none tests loss of an authorship signal.
  - **Force underdetermination**: language × force-marking interaction on bare-VP vs particle-marked items, with human instruction-hood agreement per form class (L3 C1, C2); "deictic capture" conditional on the user–agent frame (L3 C3).
  - A natively authored Vietnamese executable agent-security benchmark (the Vietnamese items found are synthetic, translated, or classification-only).
  - Any tie between person reference and obedience/safety in the Vietnamese sociolinguistic or NLP literature searched.

- Verdict: **incremental** — The specific instrument (dyad and force-marking minimal pairs in indirect injection, with a human baseline) was not found anywhere, and that part is new. But the mechanism it probes, the identity-mismatch asymmetry, the language-congruence effect and the attribution probe are each already published in English or in translated multilingual settings (2603.12277, 2610.40396, AgentDojo Table 2, XIH-Bench), and honorific register has already been tied to injection/jailbreak for Korean. As written, the cards overclaim: "new problem", "unknown whether an agent treats a directive as more authoritative because it sounds like its user", "no work on honorifics and injection", and "English cannot express the contrast" would each be contradicted by a referee with these papers in hand. The idea is defensible at ACL only if repositioned as a controlled, linguistically grounded test of the role-confusion account (one obligatory grammatical feature, user-specific cue, human ceiling, pivot erasure) with L3's force factor as the part with least prior art, and with congruence (C1/C4) demoted to replication. BabelDojo is a live competitor for the language-mismatch part.
