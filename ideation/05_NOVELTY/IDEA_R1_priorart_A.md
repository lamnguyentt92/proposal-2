  1. **arXiv:2611.15835** [full; also OpenReview UO9oWYp6W8, listed as "ICLR 2027 Conference Desk Rejected Submission", index] — Li, Hays, Ramdas, Raghavan (MIT/Stanford, 11 Nov 2026), "Controlling the Probability of Unknown Unknowns in Pre-Deployment Testing of AI Systems". *Covers:* pre-deployment testing of an AI agent framed as sequential Good–Turing missing-mass estimation; estimand R_t = probability the next session shows a failure mode not yet seen; anytime-valid upper confidence sequence (mixture of restarted betting processes), stopping rule, almost-sure halting, matching delay lower bound; extensions to (a) a sampling distribution Q_t that may be data-adaptive provided the likelihood ratio P/Q_t is known and bounded (inverse-propensity weights), (b) imperfect LLM-judge labels (PPI and PAC-label variants); experiments on SWE-Chat coding-agent traces (538 clustered failure categories, resampled with replacement) and synthetic data; cites STADS (Böhme 2018) and states that software testing targets input-space coverage whereas they target the deployment usage distribution; footnote 3 and §5 say "unseen" may be defined online by whether fixes already implemented address the failure. *Does not cover:* any adversary or attacker policy (Perez 2022 and Uesato 2018 appear only in a related-work list); sampling without a known target distribution or known propensities; number of unseen classes (richness), Chao-type lower bounds, capture–recapture or cross-campaign overlap; rarefaction or any comparison of testers/attackers; budget normalisation; prompt-injection or jailbreak data. Future work lists partial-trajectory observation, agent swarms with dependence, and a bandit version over several models, not adversaries. *Delta of R1:* adversarial search with unknown, self-chosen sampling law; estimand = size of the breakable set identified from overlap between campaigns; attacker comparison; held-out-attacker validation. *Matters to a referee?* Yes, the delta is real (their guarantee needs i.i.d. draws or known propensities, which an RL or LLM attacker does not give). But the headline framing "missing mass / Good–Turing for what testing of an AI agent did not find" is now theirs, with theory R1 does not match; R1's M1-half (per-campaign discovery probability from singletons) reads as their estimand without their guarantee. The fix-based definition of "unseen" also pre-empts part of M1's patch-equivalence species definition.   2. **arXiv:2610.40926** [full: abstract, §4.2 text around Fig. 3, App. B.4] — Song et al.   3. **arXiv:2611.12705** [full: abstract and the Overlap / Next-agent / Audit results] — Chang & Zhang (Cambridge / XSci, 9 Nov 2026), "Multi-Agent Screening: What Agreement and Acceptance Cannot Tell You". *What it does:* for several LLM agents screening items, proves that under positive dependence between agents' finds the Lincoln–Petersen quantity h10·h01/h11 is a **lower bound on the missed share** (so recall is bounded above), that under exchangeability one more agent adds at most f1/k (stated as the analogue of incidence-based Good–Turing, citing Chao & Colwell), that "an agent of a new kind is not covered", and that only auditing outside the pool closes the gap. *Delta of R1:* domain (attacks on a defended agent instead of retrieval/review screening), estimator family with intervals, experiments. *Matters to a referee?* For the "statistical guarantee" part of R1, no: S3's Approach step 4 (lower bound valid for exchangeable replicates, conservative under positive dependence) and S3-C4 (no transfer of reassurance to a different attacker) are the same statements in another domain, and both are also classical in capture–recapture. R1 cannot present the bound as new theory; it can present its use and its empirical validity under attack.

  - *Merged core, "treat a testing campaign of an AI agent as species discovery and report missing mass":* anticipated for non-adversarial pre-deployment testing by 2611.15835, with stronger theory (anytime validity, known-shift and noisy-label extensions). STADS did it for software in 2018.
  - *Merged core, "compare attackers by rarefaction":* anticipated in descriptive form by 2610.40926 (rarefaction of distinct strategies over successful attacks, matched-budget comparison, ecology citation).
  - *S3 key insight (overlap between independent campaigns speaks about the unseen) and S3 Approach step 4 (lower bound, conservative under positive dependence):* the statistical statement is in 2611.12705 for LLM agents and is classical (capture–recapture; Geer 2015 for penetration tests; software-inspection literature). Not found applied to red-team campaigns against LLMs.
  - *S3-C4 (a low discovery rate for attacker A says nothing about attacker B):* stated in general form in 2611.12705 ("an agent of a new kind is not covered"); 2611.15835's guarantee is explicitly tied to one sampling distribution.
  - *S3-C3 / M1-C2 (rankings by ASR@k differ from rankings by coverage/diversity; RL attackers collapse):* rank changes across budgets shown by Fair-ASR and OpenReview 6xcPyBfYeK; diversity collapse of RL attackers by 2611.19680 and 2610.40926; unseen-corrected rank changes in a non-adversarial setting by KnowSum. - Verdict: **incremental** — No paper found does the whole idea, and the application of unseen-species estimators to adversarial red-team campaigns with a held-out test appears unclaimed as of 2026-11-20. But each of the three pillars of the merged core has a published neighbour from the last four weeks: missing mass for AI-agent testing with real theory (2611.15835), rarefaction to compare jailbreak attackers (2610.40926), and the overlap-based lower bound under positive dependence for LLM agents (2611.12705), on top of STADS (2018) and a 2025–2026 run of "Good–Turing for LLM evaluation" papers. What remains new is an application-level step (adversarial estimand, breakable-fraction bound from campaign overlap, adaptive-bias transfer) plus an empirical validation study; the "statistical guarantee" in the contribution type is borrowed, not new. A referee is likely to accept the delta over 2611.15835 as real but to read the paper as the next instance of a known transfer unless the validation results (C1/C3-type) are strong. Residual uncertainty: OpenReview full texts, Google Scholar and the OpenAlex citation graph could not be searched, and 2611.15835's authors (ICLR 2027 desk reject, so probably resubmitting) and the SABER/CoFiBreak group are each one step from the same paper.
