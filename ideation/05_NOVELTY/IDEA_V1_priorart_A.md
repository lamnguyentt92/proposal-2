- Closest works (3–5, more if needed): identifier — what it does — delta — does the delta matter to a referee?

  1. **arXiv:2603.12277, "Prompt Injection as Role Confusion" (Ye, Cui, Hadfield-Menell; ICML 2026) [full]** — Claims and tests the general thesis V1 rests on: models attribute text to a role by how it sounds (style, lexical choice, explicit declarations, position), not by its tag; probe-measured "Userness" of a command inside a tool output predicts attack success (2% → 70% across quantiles); 212 templates vary only the declared source; a granular ablation separates lexical, **pronoun** (we/us → third person) and syntactic cues for forged reasoning. English only, no user-specific cue, no human baseline. *Delta:* V1 tests one grammaticalised, user-specific cue (the dyad the real user established) with native-authored minimal pairs and a human attribution baseline. *Matters?* Yes, but it changes the framing: V1 cannot present "agents assign authority by linguistic identity" as a new observation. A referee will read V1 as "role confusion, instantiated on a cue English lacks". The paper must cite this as the parent result and argue what the dyad adds (a cue that is obligatory, session-specific and invisible after translation).
  2. **arXiv:2610.40396 / OpenReview JVEIoRrfwW, "Understanding and Mitigating Role Spoofing in LLMs" (Edelman et al.; ICLR 2027 submission) [full; submission status index]** — Tool-output text impersonating the user; separates content-based from structure-based role tracking; a "Who Said What" task asks models who sent a snippet (the explicit self-attribution read-out of M3 item 4b); 95%+ impersonation on frontier models in AgentDojo; role probes as detector. English, no linguistic manipulation of person reference, no human baseline. *Delta:* same as above; plus V1's human-vs-model attribution gap on identical sentences. *Matters?* Yes for C2 of M3: the model-side attribution probe is no longer new, only the human baseline and the kin-term/pro-drop stratification are.
  3. **OpenReview s9A7n7Mllv, "Evaluating LLM Safety across Korean Addressee Honorification" (ACL ARR 2026 October submission, anonymous) [abs via search endpoint; PDF blocked]** — 400 HarmBench queries × four Korean speech levels (1,600 minimal variants changing only what the speech level requires), 13 LLMs, ≈120k responses; safety behaviour shifts non-monotonically along honorification and formality. *Delta:* direct harmful requests from the user, not planted third-party directives; honorific level as register, not as a marker of who is speaking; no agents, no congruence with an established dyad. *Matters?* Yes. It is a same-cycle competitor for the sentence "address/honorific forms as a minimal-pair variable in LLM compliance" and it targets the same venue. The cards' statement that no honorific work exists "on authority or injection" survives only in the narrow form "on injection / speaker attribution". The register confound that all three cards list as a risk is precisely this paper's main effect, so V1 needs a register-matched control to show the dyad effect is not a speech-level effect.
  4. **ACL Anthology 2026.oracle-main.13 / OpenReview sMz1U3eSQ7, "Does Safety Reasoning Travel? A Multilingual Measurement of Prompt-Injection Attacks and Runtime Monitors in Tool-Using LLM Agents" (Zhang; ORACLE workshop, Oct 2026) [full PDF]** — AgentDojo v1.2.1, all four suites, gpt-4o-mini and gpt-4.1-mini; the **injected instruction and the retrieved environment content** are machine-translated (NLLB-200) into French, Chinese, Arabic, Hindi, Swahili while task, tools and checker stay fixed; 320 paired scenarios per cell. ASR falls 3–6× for every non-English injection; a paraphrase control with six hand-written registers of the wrapper (original, urgent, polite, terse, procedural, apologetic) attributes 17–33% of the drop to wording; polite wrappers work best, terse worst; limitations name "register and imperative force" of the MT system and call for human translation. *Delta:* user language is not crossed (English user throughout), so congruence is confounded with "non-English"; MT, no Vietnamese, two small models, no dyad. *Matters?* Yes, in two ways. (i) It **falsifies a factual claim in all three cards** ("none varies the language of the injected content independently" / "three studies to date"): this paper does exactly that in an executable environment. (ii) Its headline result is the directional prediction of S2-C1 / M3-C4 (other-language directives are followed less), so V1's congruence claim is a completion (both directions, native text), not a discovery.
  5. **arXiv:2607.23545 / ACL Anthology 2026.emnlp-main.2153, "Language Shapes Instruction Hierarchy Compliance in Multilingual LLMs" (XIH-Bench; EMNLP 2026 main) [full pages + index]** — six languages (EN, DE, ES, FR, HI, ZH), all 36 ordered language pairs, three settings **including User > Tool and System > Tool**, 78,894 instances, 13 models; "Language Boundary Effect": cross-language conflicts are resolved in favour of the higher-priority source more often than same-language ones (2.2–4.0 pp, all 15 pairs positive; item-level regression ≈2.4 pp). LLM-translated, mostly single-turn, no Vietnamese, language as label. *Delta:* L3 already lists it and plans C4 as a replication; S2 and M3 do not list it although their C1/C4 are the same effect. *Matters?* Yes: a peer-reviewed main-conference paper already owns "matched language raises compliance with the lower-priority instruction, as an interaction". V1's congruence claim must be framed as an agentic, natively authored replication, and the expected effect size is small (a few points), which matters for power.
  6. **ACL Anthology 2026.wmt-main.16, "Evaluating Pragmatic Appropriateness in English–Vietnamese Machine Translation with Dyadic Profiles" (Huang & Tsai; WMT 2026) [abs + first pages of PDF]** — Vietnamese person reference and sender–recipient dyads as the object of study; two native annotators; a **role-reversal test** in which the same Vietnamese text is judged under intended vs reversed relationships (20/20 appropriate vs 4/20 for Gemini). Not agents, not security. *Delta:* different task. *Matters?* Moderately: it removes "first use of Vietnamese dyads with native role-reversal judgments in LLM evaluation" from the list of possible firsts, it is the closest methodological precedent for the annotator protocol, and its finding that LLM translations *without* profiles were already rated appropriate weakens the assumption behind the "machine-translated Vietnamese" baseline (MT output may not be as dyad-broken as the cards expect).
  7. **arXiv:2607.01153, "Adversarial Pragmatics for AI Safety Evaluation" (Reynolds) [full pages]** — taxonomy of instruction status under embedding, mention/use, scope, **deixis**, indirect speech acts; 18 English seed items incl. one tool-result contrast; states that deixis, politeness and source attribution "vary across languages" and each move needs new adjudication. *Delta:* English, pilot scale, deixis means "the previous instruction", not addressee terms. *Matters?* Slightly; cite as the pragmatics-of-instruction-status precedent for L3.

- Parts of the idea already anticipated (be specific, claim by claim):

  - *Premise "agents assign authority by how the text sounds / who it sounds like, not by channel"* (V1 core, S2 key insight): **anticipated** in general form by 2603.12277 and 2610.40396, with mechanistic evidence and a dose–response to attack success. Not anticipated for person reference specifically.
  - *S2-C1, M3-C4, L3-C4 (language congruence as an interaction)*: **largely anticipated**. XIH-Bench shows the same-language vs cross-language effect in User>Tool and System>Tool settings over 36 language pairs; 2026.oracle-main.13 shows the drop for translated injections in an executable agent benchmark. Remaining delta: user language crossed in both directions inside an agent, native text, Vietnamese, code-switched cells.
  - *Card statements "only three studies of language and agent security exist" and "none varies the injected-content language independently"*: **false as of this search** (2026.oracle-main.13, XIH-Bench tool settings, BrowseSafe++ annotations, the Korean–English benchmark 6sY2LgIywr, and for RAG/detectors MIPIAD arXiv:2605.07269 [abs]). These sentences must be rewritten before any proposal goes out.
  - *"Honorific/kinship work exists on other tasks but none on compliance/safety"*: **partly anticipated** by s9A7n7Mllv (Korean speech levels × harmful-request compliance, minimal variants). Still unanticipated: honorific/address form as a cue to *speaker identity* in *third-party* content.
  - *M3-C2 model-side explicit attribution ("who authored this?")*: **anticipated as a method** by the "Who Said What" setting and self-attribution monitor in 2610.40396 (which also shows the self-report can be subverted by the injection, a threat to M3's read-out b). Human baseline on the same sentences: not found.
  - *L3 force factor (imperative vs declarative/polite/bare)*: **partly anticipated in English and Romance/Chinese** — Imperative Interference (trusted instructions), 2605.30686 and the register control in 2026.oracle-main.13 (polite > terse for injected text), DACSI arXiv:2606.09005 [abs] (non-imperative payloads). The Vietnamese-specific prediction (gap concentrated on particle-less bare VPs; log line vs command) was not found.
  - *L3 benign look-alikes / over-execution arm*: the general idea (benign instruction-like text, detector over-defence) is established (PIGuard/NotInject 2025.acl-long.1468 [index], PIDS-Bench arXiv:2609.15017 [index], per-instruction compliance 2610.22874 [abs]); not in Vietnamese and not tied to grammatical force.
  - *Non-malicious directives as the instrument* (S2 item 1): taken from 2611.11861 [abs], as the card says.
  - *Vietnamese dyads judged by native speakers under matched vs reversed relationship*: **anticipated as an evaluation device** in MT by 2026.wmt-main.16.

- Parts with no prior work found:

  - A planted directive whose **self/addressee terms match vs mismatch the dyad the user established** with the assistant, with content, position and language held fixed (S2-C2, M3-C1, L3-C3 "deictic capture"). No paper found in any source that manipulates address terms, kin terms, T/V forms or honorifics *inside injected or tool-returned content*, in any language.
  - Vietnamese in any agent-security or prompt-injection study (only 46 in-the-wild Vietnamese injections inside BrowseSafe++, unanalysed; SEATauBench is capability only).
  - A **human attribution baseline** ("user or someone else wrote this sentence?") against which model attribution and behaviour are scored.
  - The **language × force-marking interaction** for a language without a morphological imperative (L3-C1/C2), including human agreement on instruction-hood per form class.
  - **English-pivot erasure of a provenance cue** (S2-C4, M3-C3): the pivot is documented (2608.11110), translate-then-guard is discussed, but nobody tests that translating to English removes an authorship signal and changes agent or guard behaviour.
  - Detectors/guards scored on dyad-matched vs dyad-mismatched items (S2-C3).

- Verdict: novel | incremental | anticipated — reason

  **novel** — for the core instrument and its two headline predictions (dyad congruence; force underdetermination in Vietnamese): 171 arXiv queries, 95 OpenReview queries, a 25,599-title Anthology scan and 14 semantic searches produced no work that manipulates person reference or address forms in injected content, and no Vietnamese agent-security study. The verdict is narrow. The surrounding claims are **incremental**: the general thesis is Role Confusion (ICML 2026) and Role Spoofing (ICLR 2027 submission); the language-congruence claims are XIH-Bench (EMNLP 2026) plus "Does Safety Reasoning Travel?" (ORACLE 2026); honorific level as a compliance variable is the Korean ARR October 2026 submission. A referee who knows those four will accept the paper only if the dyad result is the centrepiece, the congruence result is presented as replication, and a register-matched control separates "sounds like the user" from "speech level". A search that finds nothing is not proof: non-public ARR and NeurIPS submissions, and OpenReview PDFs, were out of reach.
