- Closest works (3–5, more if needed): identifier — what it does — delta — does the delta matter to a referee?

  **A. Seeded-fault estimator (T1 arm)**

  1. **Mills, "On the Statistical Validation of Computer Programs", IBM FSD report FSC-72-6015, 1972** — [primary NOT obtained; verified through three secondary/authoritative records: Pfleeger's textbook excerpt in Dr. Dobb's Journal, March 1998 (page read); Duran & Wiorkowski, IEEE TSE SE-7(1):147–148, 1981, DOI 10.1109/tse.1981.230821 (abstract read); Knight & Ammann, NASA NTRS 19860020890, 1985 (record + abstract read)]. Seed S known faults, let the test process find s of them and n indigenous ones, estimate indigenous total N = S·n/s; Duran & Wiorkowski add confidence limits and ML estimates and call it capture–recapture. T1's FP_hat = n·s_surv/(1−s_surv) is this estimator, algebraically. Delta of T1: the "tester" is a benchmark grader, the "faults" are wrong end-states, strata are failure modes, and the output is per agent. **Delta matters only modestly: a referee who knows Mills will call the statistical core textbook; the novelty has to sit in the stratification, the agent-specific use and the validation.** Pfleeger states the standing objection directly: the method presumes seeded faults match real ones in kind and difficulty, which is hard to guarantee because the typical real faults are unknown until found. Knight & Ammann tested the assumptions on 17 programs / 408 seeded versions: seeded and indigenous errors were about equally hard to find, but "all errors of one type are equally hard" failed, and seeded errors interfered with indigenous ones.

  2. **Rogan & Gladen, Am. J. Epidemiol. 107(1):71–76, 1978, DOI 10.1093/oxfordjournals.aje.a112510** [record + abstract via scilit; PubMed blocked] and its 2025–2026 ports to LLM evaluation: **arXiv 2511.21140** (Lee et al., "How to Correctly Report LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluations") [abstract]; **arXiv 2601.05420** (Chen et al., Rogan–Gladen vs prediction-powered inference, efficiency theory) [abstract]; **arXiv 2605.06939** (Fiedler, shared calibration across compared models can flip the sign of a comparison) [abstract]; **arXiv 2609.34198** (judge error depends on agent version; transporting old calibration raised comparison error from 3.8 to 19.5 points) [abstract]; **arXiv 2606.10315** (production transaction agent: judge sensitivity measured against exhaustive human review, Rogan–Gladen applied, 3–6x undercount implied, and the correction degenerates when the apparent rate is zero) [abstract]. These already do "measure the grader's sensitivity, then correct the observed rate, with intervals" for agent/LLM grading. Delta of T1: sensitivity comes from *seeded* end-states with no human-labelled agent runs, the grader is programmatic, and the correction is stratified by failure mode. **Delta matters, but T1's own baseline B3 is this method; a referee will see T1 as "Rogan–Gladen with spiked-sample sensitivity and specificity assumed 1", and will ask about both omissions (see anticipated parts).**

  3. **Sun, "Mutation Testing of Task-Scoped State Oracles in Software-Agent Benchmarks: A Cross-Benchmark Empirical Study", Research Square preprint rs-10665114 v1, DOI 10.21203/rs.3.rs-10665114/v1, posted 2026-08-18** [abstract page read; not on arXiv, not in either idea card]. Injects six classes of harmful state faults plus three benign transformations into known-valid executions of τ²-Bench, ToolSandbox and AppWorld and re-scores with the official evaluators, no model calls: 100 of 110 harmful mutations rejected (90.9%, Wilson interval reported), all ten misses are collateral changes in ToolSandbox. This is T1 steps 1–3 (agent-free, state-level, fault-class-stratified seeding with a kill rate and interval) on agent benchmarks. Delta: no mobile benchmark, no conversion of the kill rate into a count of false passes for real agents, mutants not derived from agent failures. **Delta matters — it is exactly the estimator step — but this paper removes "state-level seeding of agent graders" from T1's novelty list and must be cited.**

  4. **arXiv 2610.09142** (Abrich, AppWorld/WorkArena side-effect loopholes) [full text] and **arXiv 2609.22220** (Du et al., KernelBench-M) [full text]. Both already in T1's card; what the full texts add: (i) 2610.09142 says its counts give "no prevalence estimate", that "a rate needs a declared sampling frame", proposes publishing a per-template mutation score beside the leaderboard and measuring recall on planted loopholes as future work, and reports that variants of a template received the same verdict in 107 of 109 template-by-edit groups; (ii) 2609.22220 has a per-family kill matrix with a witnessed denominator and explicitly restricts itself to comparative and existence claims — its realism probe is 60 LLM-written kernels with three bugs. Neither computes false accepts for a leaderboard. **Delta (estimator + validation against audit) matters and is real, but the next step is spelled out in 2610.09142's own future-work section, so scoop risk is high.**

  5. **GitHub issue xlang-ai/OSWorld#599, "Some checkers accept wrong answers, or pass with no agent action" (opened 2026-09-26, closed)** [issue page read]. Plants one wrong value in the field each task asks the agent to fix: 53 of 255 planted-wrong values across 65 gold-file checks still score above 0 (about 21%), 13 checkers implicated; adds a null-agent baseline (3 tasks pass with no action) and a 100-run flakiness check. Also **google-research/android_world#380** (opened 2026-09-23, open): the list-answer checker accepts one correct item repeated N times; six AndroidWorld information-retrieval tasks affected; found by code reading, no rate estimate [issue page read]. Delta: issue-tracker evidence, no estimator, no agent runs. **Matters as evidence that the seeding instrument has been run on a computer-use benchmark by practitioners and that AndroidWorld false passes are already being reported one checker at a time.**

  **B. Metamorphic inverse-pair instrument (M2 arm)**

  6. **Adamsen, Mezzetti, Møller, "Systematic Execution of Android Test Suites in Adverse Conditions" (Thor), ISSTA 2015** [full text]. Injects "neutral event sequences" (pause–resume, pause–stop–restart, destroy–create, request–abandon audio focus) that should leave the outcome unchanged; a failing existing assertion after injection is the bug signal, so no new expected output is written. Related Android oracles of the same family: **Riganelli et al., Data Loss Detector, arXiv 2010.05584** [abstract] (state must survive stop/restart; two oracle strategies; 75% of 110 known data-loss faults revealed); **Su et al., Genie, OOPSLA 2021, arXiv 2008.03585** [abstract] (independent-view property, "inspired by metamorphic testing", property-preserving mutant tests); **Sun et al., PBFDroid, ESEC/FSE 2023** [full text] (model-based create/read/update/delete/search properties checked against GUI state; no round-trip or undo property); **Zaeem, Prasad, Khurshid, ICST 2014, DOI 10.1109/ICST.2014.31** [record only — content not read]. Delta of M2: these test the *app* using an assumed-correct property; M2 turns the same "do-and-undo must be a no-op" relation on the *agent's runs* to expose the *benchmark checker's* blind spot, with a measured noise mask and a prevalence bound. **Delta matters. But the relation itself (inverse/neutral sequences return Android state) is established Android-testing practice — claim the target and the estimand, not the relation.**

  7. **Hughes, "How to Specify It! A Guide to Writing Properties of Pure Functions", LNCS 12053:58–83, 2020** [full text]. Textbook-level statement of metamorphic properties over state-changing operations: insert/delete and insert/insert relations (inverse-like and commutation), with a seeded-bug comparison of postcondition, metamorphic and model-based properties (eight seeded bugs; individual metamorphic properties miss bugs but are strong in combination; model-based most effective). Delta: pure data structures, not agents or graders. **Matters for framing: M2's four relations (round-trip, idempotence, commutation, pre-satisfied goal) are standard property classes; the lineage citation should be this plus Chen et al., ACM CSUR 51(1) 4:1–4:27, 2018 [record + abstract].**

  8. **arXiv 2609.01354** (Xin, "Where the Verifier Fails") [abstract]. Applies metamorphic testing *to the verifier rather than the model*: certified-equivalent rewrites of correct maths answers, so each rejection is a provable false negative with no human adjudication; 307,420 verdicts over four verifiers. Same logical move as M2 (a relation certifies a wrong verdict label-free), opposite error direction, text answers, no environment state. Also **arXiv 2608.03337** (Pei et al.) [abstract]: inferred metamorphic relations used to *assess UI test suites' oracles* ("MR coverage", weak-oracle cases). **Delta (false passes, stateful environment, relations between tasks, real agent runs) matters, but "metamorphic relations audit the oracle without labels" is no longer a first.**

  9. **arXiv 2601.06112** (ReliabilityBench) [full text]. "Action metamorphic relations" for tool agents with correctness defined by end-state equivalence. The relations are instruction perturbations only (synonym, paraphrase, reordering, distractor, date format); they test the agent, and no checker error is estimated. **Delta is large; cite to pre-empt "metamorphic relations on agent end-states already exist".** Agent-side neighbours that do not audit checkers: UndoBench arXiv 2610.05622 [abstract], SABench arXiv 2610.34167 [abstract] (12.8% of goal-satisfied runs still make unauthorised writes, measured with hand-built per-task authorisation oracles), Agent-Diff arXiv 2602.11224 and EffectMatch arXiv 2609.31301 [abstracts], arXiv 2610.16067 [full text] (five state-integrity invariants on the *oracle's own* statefulness, e.g. a call must not change graded state and repeated calls must agree — idempotence of the grader, not relations between tasks).

  **C. SWE-bench false-pass audits (analogue for both arms)**

  10. **arXiv 2410.06992** SWE-Bench+ [abstract]: manual screening of the passes of one agent; 32.67% solution leakage and 31.08% weak-test passes; resolution 12.47% → 3.97%. **arXiv 2503.15223** PatchDiff (ICSE 2026 per the reference list of 2610.09142) [abstract]: differential patch testing against the gold patch plus manual inspection; 29.6% of plausible patches behave differently, 28.6% of those certainly wrong; inflation 6.2 points. **arXiv 2506.09289** UTBoost [abstract]: LLM test augmentation; 345 wrongly passed patches; 18 and 11 ranking changes. **arXiv 2603.00520** SWE-ABS [abstract]: coverage-driven plus mutation-driven adversarial tests; 19.71% of passing patches rejected; top agent 78.80% → 62.20%. **arXiv 2610.10619** TestJack [abstract]: per-trial generated tests kept only if the gold patch passes; about 34.4% of passes violate requirements. **arXiv 2609.34262** (Scale AI) [abstract + first page]: LLM process audit of 3,810 passing trajectories; defines the "integrity gap" as the share of unearned passes. **METR note 2026-03-10** [page read]: four maintainers review 296 test-passing PRs from five agents; about half would not be merged; correction by normalising to the golden-patch merge rate. All of these **re-grade the passes with a stronger oracle** (more tests, differential testing, human or LLM review). None estimates false passes indirectly from the failures plus a seeded detection rate, and none uses relations between tasks. **How a referee compares: "the coding community got its numbers by building a stronger oracle and re-running — why is an indirect estimator needed when emulator post-state can simply be re-checked?" T1's B1 baseline answers this only if the estimator is clearly cheaper per new agent at equal accuracy; the SWE-bench line also shows the expected reporting format (rate among passes, corrected score, rank changes).**

- Parts of the idea already anticipated (be specific, claim by claim; treat the fault-seeding estimator and the metamorphic inverse-pair instrument separately):

  **Fault-seeding estimator**
  - *The estimator formula.* Fully anticipated: Mills 1972 / Lincoln–Petersen, with interval theory in Duran & Wiorkowski 1981. T1's Horvitz–Thompson form adds nothing mathematically. T1's own source-status note is still open: I could not obtain the Mills report either (it is an IBM technical report; the TRACE Mills Collection index pages 2–4 that I opened do not list it; secondary accounts cite a reprint in Mills, *Software Productivity*, 1983, which I did not see). Cite via Pfleeger's textbook and Duran & Wiorkowski until the reprint is read.
  - *Correcting an observed rate with measured grader sensitivity, with intervals.* Anticipated for LLM judges (2511.21140, 2601.05420) and already applied to an agent grader with the "3–6x undercount" reading (2606.10315). The degenerate case T1 calls "blind modes" is the same degeneracy 2606.10315 reports for a zero apparent rate.
  - *Agent-free, state-level, class-stratified seeding of agent-benchmark graders with a kill rate and interval.* Anticipated by Sun (Research Square rs-10665114), by 2610.09142 (hand-written), by OSWorld#599 (issue tracker) and, outside agents, by 2609.22220's kill matrix. T1's card lists only two of these.
  - *"Mutant must be confirmed harmful before entering the denominator."* Anticipated: witnessed denominator (2609.22220), independent end-state confirmation (2610.09142), harmful vs benign classes (Sun).
  - *The single assumption and its classic criticism.* Anticipated and unresolved. Evidence a referee will bring: Pfleeger (seeded faults must match real ones in kind and complexity); Knight & Ammann 1985 (equal difficulty within a type does not hold); Just et al., FSE 2014 [full text] — 73% of 357 real faults are coupled to mutants, 27% are not, and 17% (algorithmic changes, code deletion) are coupled to no mutant at all, with the authors declining to interpret absolute mutation scores; Petrović et al., ICSE 2021, arXiv 2103.07189 [abstract] (coupling shown at scale, again as association, not as an estimator); the diagnostic-testing "spectrum effect" (Usher-Smith, Sharp, Griffin, BMJ 2016;353:i3139 [full text]; originally Ransohoff & Feinstein 1978 [listing only]) — sensitivity measured on one case mix does not transfer to another; and, in LLM evaluation, 2605.06939 and 2609.34198, which show calibration transported across models or versions can reverse comparisons. **Nobody has "solved" representativeness.** What the literature offers, and what a referee will expect to see: stratify by fault class (T1 does this); derive seeds from observed real faults (T1 does this); validate coupling on a labelled sample of real faults and report it (T1's decisive experiment); report bounds rather than a point when independence fails (Lam & Stork, IJCAI 2003, pp. 513–517 [abstract], give upper and lower bounds on true error when labeller and classifier are dependent; 2605.10448 already reports partial-identification bounds for AndroidWorld). I found no published bound specific to seeded-versus-real detectability.
  - *Two gaps the prior art exposes in T1's design.* (a) T1 treats every grader FAIL as a true failure. Rogan–Gladen needs specificity as well, and 2607.28367 (abstract) reports 15.3% of FAIL verdicts wrong, 10.7% being evaluator false negatives; the n in n·s/(1−s) is therefore inflated unless false FAILs are removed first. (b) Programmatic checkers are deterministic: 2610.09142 found identical verdicts in 107 of 109 template-by-edit groups, and 2609.22220 stresses that its misses are deterministic. If survival per (task, mode) cell is essentially 0 or 1, cells with s=0 contribute nothing and cells with s=1 are "not estimable" by T1's own rule, so the estimator works only through heterogeneity inside a mode — which is exactly where Knight & Ammann found the equal-difficulty assumption to fail.
  - *Unsampled alternatives a referee may name.* Two-inspector capture–recapture with no seeding at all (programmatic checker and a state-reading LLM judge as two "inspectors"): the inspection literature is Briand, El Emam, Freimut, Laitenberger, IEEE TSE 2000 [record only] and Petersson et al., JSS 2004 [listing only]; species-estimator versions for imperfect detectors exist in data cleaning (Chung, Krishnan, Kraska, arXiv 1611.04878 [abstract]). I found no application of either to agent graders.

  **Metamorphic inverse-pair instrument**
  - *Inverse / neutral sequences as an oracle on Android state.* Anticipated in app testing (Thor, DLD, Genie; PBFDroid for CRUD postconditions).
  - *Round-trip, commutation and idempotence as metamorphic property classes for stateful operations.* Anticipated (Hughes 2020; Chen et al. 2018 for the method family).
  - *Using a metamorphic relation to certify a verifier's wrong verdict with no human label.* Anticipated for false negatives of maths answer checkers (2609.01354); MR-based assessment of oracle strength exists for UI component tests (2608.03337); LLM judges (SANE, 2610.28886 [abstract]).
  - *End-state-equivalence metamorphic relations in agent benchmarks.* Anticipated in name (2601.06112), for instruction perturbations only.
  - *"Compare the whole end state with a reference; costs are fields that legitimately vary and several valid end states."* Stated as future work in 2610.09142 [full text]; M2's noise mask and round-trip reference are the unbuilt answer. Hand-specified versions exist (Agent-Diff, EffectMatch, SABench).
  - *Planted-fault validation of the instrument (M2 claim C4).* Standard practice (Hughes; Sun; 2610.16067).

  - Either instrument on a mobile / Android agent benchmark. The only mobile-specific items found are AndroidWorld issue reports (#380 read; #365, #366 on vacuous task generation and #393 on an impossible verifier seen as titles only) and LLM-judge reliability (MobileJudgeBench, arXiv 2608.11434 [abstract]); the evidence-bounds audit 2605.10448 includes AndroidWorld but by per-record review.

- Verdict: **incremental** — reason: The statistical core of the seeding arm is fifty-year-old textbook material (Mills seeding = Lincoln–Petersen; Rogan–Gladen for the corrected rate) and has already been ported to LLM and agent grading; state-level seeding of agent-benchmark graders with kill rates already exists in at least four 2026 sources, one of them (Sun, Research Square) missing from the cards. The relations in the metamorphic arm are established Android-testing and property-based-testing practice, and "metamorphic relations audit the verifier without labels" has a 2026 precedent in the opposite error direction. What I could not find anywhere is the combination that F1 actually claims: an agent-specific false-pass estimate obtained without reading the passes, a label-free lower bound from inverse-task pairs on real agent runs, both on a mobile benchmark and both validated against a blind audit. So the idea is not anticipated, but each ingredient is, and the contribution a referee will credit is (i) the validation of the seeded-versus-real assumption, (ii) the cross-task relations applied to checker audit, and (iii) the mobile findings — not the estimator. The metamorphic arm is the more defensible of the two as "new"; the seeding arm is the more exposed, both to "this is Mills/Rogan–Gladen" and to the determinism and false-FAIL problems noted above.
