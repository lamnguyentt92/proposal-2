## - Closest works for the NEW elements (identifier — what it does — delta — does the delta matter to a referee?):

**Element 3 / claim C2 (most important finds; none is on the idea card)**

1. **AmbiCoref — Yuan, Malaviya, Yatskar, Findings of EACL 2023 (`2023.findings-eacl.75`) [ABS].** A diagnostic corpus of minimal sentence pairs with ambiguous vs unambiguous pronoun referents built on a *report frame* ("Abby told Brittney that she upset / hugged Courtney"). Human judgements show lower certainty on the ambiguous member; most coreference models barely change their output between the two members.
   — *Delta:* English third-person pronouns; coreference models, not memory writers; no kin terms, no dyad, no "about-whom / source chain" record, no information-matched protocol across a bounded window.
   — *Matters?* **Yes.** The diagnostic layer's "chị Lan bảo chị mệt" / "anh Nam bảo anh…" pairs are the Vietnamese kin-term analogue of this design (minimal pairs in a telling frame + human uncertainty vs model commitment). The *design* is therefore precedent, not contribution; the contribution is the linguistic content (open-class kin terms anchored to the reported dyad) and the memory-write setting. Must be cited as the design ancestor.

2. **Correct-Detect — Shore, Scheinberg, Agrawal, Lee, EMNLP 2025 (`2025.emnlp-main.1527`) [ABS].** Reports that LLMs can do coreference disambiguation and can detect coreference ambiguity, but not both together, and names this the "CORRECT-DETECT trade-off".
   — *Delta:* English pronoun coreference, prompt-level tasks, no memory, no graded rater distribution, no retention measure.
   — *Matters?* **Yes — it anticipates the shape of C2.** C2's second half ("abstention lowers silent commitment only by also dropping facts raters agree on") is the same trade-off, measured with a different yardstick. A referee can call C2 a re-discovery unless the paper cites this and states what is new: (a) the trade-off is priced in *licensed-fact retention* against a human "cannot tell" distribution, (b) inside production memory writers, (c) on a reference system where ambiguity is structural rather than incidental.

3. **"It Depends: Resolving Referential Ambiguity in Minimal Contexts with Commonsense Knowledge" — Ellinger & Groh, UncertaiNLP 2025 (`2025.uncertainlp-main.20`) [ABS].** On a multilingual multi-turn set, LLMs facing a persistently ambiguous reference tend to pick one reading or enumerate all, rather than hedge or ask.
   — *Delta:* response generation, not record writing; languages not stated in the abstract; no kin terms.
   — *Matters?* Moderately. It is an existing name for "silent commitment" in conversation. Cite; do not present silent commitment as a newly observed behaviour — present it as newly *measured at write time* where it becomes persistent.

4. **Hlava Cor / Hlava AD — Nedoluzhko et al., 2026 (`2026.slide-1.21`, arXiv:2606.25383) [ABS].** Czech coreference with three parallel annotators and explanations; about 60–65% agreement; agreement is lower where automatic resolvers disagree.
   — *Delta:* Czech, classical coreference, three annotators, no "cannot tell" mass used as gold, no LLM memory.
   — *Matters?* Somewhat: it shows that distributional gold for coreference already exists as a resource type, so "graded gold" is a method choice with precedent (together with Phrase Detectives, mentioned in `2026.lrec-1.144` [ABS]). No source found applies human label variation to memory / fact extraction — that application is still open.

5. **"Learning to Reject with a Fixed Predictor: Application to Decontextualization" — arXiv:2301.09044 (ICLR 2024 per OpenReview index) [ABS].** A reject-option learner evaluated on decontextualization with 2,000 manually labelled examples.
   — *Delta:* English, sentence-level, theory paper; rejection is on predictor error, not on referential indeterminacy.
   — *Matters?* It strengthens the card's own remark that "contextualise-then-extract with abstention" is the baseline family a referee will demand: abstention for decontextualization already has a published formulation.

**Element 2 (decontextualization / perspective shift)**

6. **DIAL-SUMMER — Ramnath et al., arXiv:2602.08149 [FT].** Error taxonomy and 192 annotated summaries of human–assistant dialogues (Anthropic test set, Llama-3.3-70B summaries) built explicitly around the shift from first/second-person talk to third-person narration; includes SPEAKER MISATTRIBUTION and VIEWPOINT DISTORTION (a statement by a speaker written as plain fact; found in 20.8% of summaries); two annotators per item with author tie-breaking; LLM judges tested. The limitations section states the work is English-only.
   — *Delta:* English, two speakers labelled "Human" / "Assistant", no third parties reported inside turns, no kin terms, categorical labels, summaries rather than atomic memory records.
   — *Matters?* Yes for positioning: "turning dialogue into third-person text creates person and viewpoint errors" is already a named, annotated phenomenon for user–assistant dialogue. L1S must claim only the part English cannot show (the person step is non-trivial when person is not lexical).

7. **"He Said, She Said: Style Transfer for Shifting the Perspective of Dialogues" — Bertsch et al., arXiv:2210.15462 [ABS]; CONFIT, arXiv:2112.08713 [ABS].** The first defines "perspective shift" (first-person dialogue → formal third person) as a task needing coreference resolution; the second gives a typology of factual errors in dialogue summaries. Both English (SAMSum-based).
   — *Delta / matters?* Same as item 6; these are the older anchors. No non-English, pro-drop or kin-term follow-up was found in Anthology, arXiv or OpenReview searches.

8. **Non-English decontextualization / claim extraction.** Found only: Czech and Slovak claim-extraction metrics (arXiv:2511.14566 [ABS]); Molecular Facts (arXiv:2406.20079 [ABS], English). **No record found** of decontextualization or standalone-fact extraction evaluated for person-reference errors in any non-English language.

**Element 1 (kin-term person reference; Vietnamese coreference)**

9. **"Coreference Resolution for Vietnamese Narrative Texts" — Tran, Nguyen, Nguyen (UIT), arXiv:2504.19606 [FT].** 266 first-person "confession"-style narratives from VnExpress, person mentions only, annotated by volunteers with SACR; GPT-3.5-Turbo vs GPT-4 with three few-shot documents on *gold mentions* (cluster the given spans): CoNLL F1 0.478 vs 0.735. Examples include kin terms used as person forms ("[Em]#1 trân trọng…", "[bố tôi]", "anh" for the husband). No agreement figure, no analysis by mention type.
   — *Delta:* written monologue, not dialogue; no reported-speech or dyad analysis; no error category for kin terms; no ambiguity / "cannot tell"; models two generations old; no memory.
   — *Matters?* **Yes, in two ways.** (i) It is the only Vietnamese coreference set with an LLM evaluation found, so the card's "not found … any Vietnamese [resource]" must be narrowed to *memory-write / dyad-anchored* resources. (ii) It is usable as an external check: a referee may ask whether a frontier model simply solves Vietnamese person coreference on this set — a cheap sanity run.

10. **KoCoNovel, arXiv:2404.01140 [ABS]** — Korean literary character coreference; reports that 24% of character mentions are bare common nouns and links this to Korean address-term practice. **ThaiCoref, arXiv:2406.06000 [ABS]** and **Z-coref (`2024.acl-srw.56`) [ABS]** — Thai coreference / zero-pronoun resources with "Thai-specific" scheme adjustments. **BanglaSocialBench (`2026.acl-srw.22`) [ABS]** — 1,719 items on Bangla address terms, kinship reasoning and customs; twelve LLMs default to over-formal address and conflate kin terms (generation / choice, not resolution). **"Recognized but Not Produced", arXiv:2609.26942 [ABS]** — kin-term generation in Hindi, Tamil, Korean. **Javanese honorifics, arXiv:2502.20864 [ABS]**.
    — *Delta:* all are either classical coreference corpora (no LLM memory, no reported dyad) or generation-side appropriateness. None asks "to whom does this kin term refer inside a report". 
    — *Matters?* These supply the "second kin-term language" a referee may ask about (Korean and Bangla have ready material) and should be cited as the neighbouring line; they do not pre-empt.

11. **Linguistics, for the theory paragraph:** Yonezawa (2024), *Linguistics*, DOI 10.1515/ling-2022-0041 [ABS] — generic / vague *anata* in Japanese reported speech in an "open-class person-reference system"; argues these uses are the quoting speaker's construction. Charoenporn & Sornlertlamvanich (2023), "Using of Kinship Terms in Pronoun Substitute and Address Terms in the Thai Language", IEEE IES, DOI 10.1109/ies59143.2023.10242421 [IDX]. Useful vocabulary ("open-class person reference", "pronoun substitute"); not NLP evaluations.

**Element 5 (diacritics in memory tools)**

12. **getzep/graphiti PR #1357 (opened 29 Mar 2026, open) and PR #1850 (opened 8 Sep 2026, open) [PR]; `dedup_helpers.py` on `main` [CODE].** Both PRs state that `_normalize_name_for_fuzzy` removes everything outside `[a-z0-9' ]`. #1357 frames it for CJK (similarity 0.0, pairs fall through to the LLM: cost / latency, no wrong merge claimed). #1850 frames it for Korean, with Japanese, Cyrillic, Greek and accented Latin listed; its proposed fix *folds* accents ("Ångström" → "angstrom"). Neither mentions Vietnamese, and neither reports distinct entities being merged. The regex is still on `main` today (line 48).
    — *Delta:* the defect is publicly reported, but only as a *missed fast path*. The Vietnamese-specific consequence is different in kind: because Vietnamese names mix ASCII and non-ASCII letters, the stripped form is non-empty and passes the entropy gate.
    — *Matters?* **Yes, the card must change its wording.** "Graphiti's name deduplication strips non-ASCII letters" is a known, twice-reported fact with open fixes — cite the PRs. What remains unreported is the false-merge consequence for Vietnamese. Helper-level arithmetic done this session (my own re-implementation of the regex and of the gate constants read from `main`: entropy ≥ 1.5, Jaccard ≥ 0.9; **not** an end-to-end Graphiti run): "Trần Thị Hạnh" / "Trần Thị Hành" both normalise to `tr n th h nh`; "Lê Thị Mỹ Hằng" / "Lê Thị Mỹ Hồng" to `l th m h ng`; "Phạm Minh Tú" / "Phạm Minh Tứ" to `ph m minh t` — identical strings, Jaccard 1.00, entropy 1.9–2.5. "Nguyễn Văn Lân" / "Nguyễn Văn Lan" do *not* collide (Jaccard 0.67). So the collapse is predicted for names that differ only in a non-ASCII letter at the same position, not for every diacritic difference. Two timing risks: if #1850 is merged, accent folding would make the collision *broader* (Lan / Lân / Lấn → `lan`), and the engineering check must then be reported against a pinned commit.

13. **mem0ai/mem0 issue #4884 [PR-level page read].** Reports that Mem0 v3's BM25 and entity-boost paths load spaCy `en_core_web_sm`, so non-English text silently degrades to semantic-only retrieval; examples in Chinese, Japanese, Hindi; Thai, Korean, Arabic listed. No Vietnamese, no pronouns, no extraction-prompt discussion.
    — *Matters?* Minor; supports G12 ("English-designed pipeline") on the retrieval side. Also seen in the index only: graphiti issue #804 (Korean prompt corruption from `ensure_ascii`, closed) [IDX].

## - Anything that anticipates a claim (C1–C4) or the key insight:

- **Key insight (dyad-anchored deixis resolution as the hidden step of memory writing; kin-noun literalisation and dyad capture as its error types):** nothing found. No paper, issue or benchmark found evaluates resolution of a kin term to a person through the reported speech event, in any language, for memory, summarisation, IE, DST or translation.
- **C1 (information-matched divergence on kin-term items):** not anticipated. No memory or extraction study was found that gives raters exactly the writer's input window. Hlava Cor and AmbiCoref are precedents for *comparing models with human uncertainty*, not for the information-matched protocol.
- **C2 (silent commitment; abstention costs retention):** **partly anticipated in English coreference.** AmbiCoref (models insensitive where humans are unsure), Correct-Detect (cannot disambiguate and detect ambiguity at once), "It Depends" (commit to one reading instead of hedging). C2 survives as a new *measurement* (retention-priced, against a rater distribution, in memory writers, on kin terms) but not as a new *phenomenon*. Note also the predecessor finding (Manufactured Confidence, hedge-preserving prompt without over-suppression) already cuts the other way; with Correct-Detect the literature now has evidence on both sides, which makes C2 a well-posed test rather than a foregone result.
- **C3 (English pivot relocates errors):** not anticipated. No translate-then-extract study with person-assignment scoring was found for any kin-term language. (WMT 2026 `2026.wmt-main.16`, already on the card, is EN→VI generation.)
- **C4 (it occurs):** no competing measurement exists — see next section.
- **Engineering check (Graphiti):** the *cause* is anticipated by PR #1357 and PR #1850; the *Vietnamese false-merge effect* is not.

## - Verdict for the redesigned idea: novel | incremental | anticipated — reason

**Novel (core), with one claim to be re-scoped as incremental.**

- The core — kin-term person resolution through the reported dyad at memory-write time, its two specific error types (kin-noun literalisation, dyad capture), the information-matched human comparison, the English-pivot test, and the Vietnamese elicitation study — was not found published in whole or in part across alphaXiv, arXiv, ACL Anthology (all years), OpenReview, Crossref, GitHub and web searches.
- **C2 is incremental as a phenomenon**: silent commitment under referential ambiguity and the disambiguate-vs-detect trade-off are established for English coreference (AmbiCoref 2023; Correct-Detect, EMNLP 2025; "It Depends" 2025). Keep C2, cite these, and claim the measurement (retention cost against a rater distribution inside memory writers), not the discovery.
- **The diagnostic-layer design is precedented** by AmbiCoref (minimal pairs in a telling frame with human uncertainty); **graded coreference gold is precedented** by Hlava Cor / Phrase Detectives. Present both as adopted methodology.
- **The Graphiti statement must be corrected**: the non-ASCII stripping is already reported in two open PRs; only the Vietnamese false-merge consequence is new, it holds for a subset of name pairs, and it is time-sensitive (pin the commit; re-check if #1850 merges).
- **The "no Vietnamese resource" statement must be narrowed**: arXiv:2504.19606 is a Vietnamese person-coreference set with an LLM evaluation; it is not memory-related and has no dyad analysis.

Card edits recommended: add AmbiCoref, Correct-Detect, "It Depends", DIAL-SUMMER, arXiv:2504.19606, Hlava Cor, arXiv:2301.09044, Graphiti PRs #1357 / #1850 and Mem0 #4884 to "Closest work"; reword C2 and the Graphiti sentence as above; consider KoCoNovel or BanglaSocialBench material for the planned second language.
