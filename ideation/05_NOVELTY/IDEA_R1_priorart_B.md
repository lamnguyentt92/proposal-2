## Closest works (3–5, more if needed): identifier — what it does — delta — does the delta matter to a referee?

1. **Böhme, "STADS: Software Testing as Species Discovery", arXiv 1803.02130 / TOSEM 27(2), 2018 [full, selected pages]; with Böhme, Liyanage, Wüstholz, "Estimating Residual Risk in Greybox Fuzzing", ESEC/FSE 2021, doi 10.1145/3468264.3468570 [abs].**
   - What it does: frames a security testing campaign as species sampling; discovery probability (Good-Turing) as the residual-risk guarantee when nothing was found; Chao1 richness and species coverage for campaign completeness; an incidence (Bernoulli-product) model with sampling units; extrapolation to more effort; comparison of tools by completeness; explicit treatment of adaptive sampling bias (the 2021 paper gives a probabilistic analysis and two estimator classes that correct it). STADS is positioned in the security domain (its CCS concept is "Penetration testing") and states that its guarantees hold only with respect to the specific fuzzer, and that systematic (non-repeating) search violates the model outright.
   - Delta: the system under test is an LLM agent, the sampler is an LLM/RL attacker, and species have no natural coverage element. The estimator suite, the residual-risk reading, the adaptive-bias problem and the "guarantee is relative to the sampler" caveat (M1's key insight and half of S3's C4) are all already there.
   - Does the delta matter? Only if the paper shows the transfer is non-trivial: that the fuzzing corrections fail or need changing for LLM attackers, or that species definitions can be validated. A referee who knows STADS will read the method section as a port. Both cards already cite it; the report here confirms how much of the framing it owns.

2. **Li, Hays, Ramdas, Raghavan, "Controlling the Probability of Unknown Unknowns in Pre-Deployment Testing of AI Systems", arXiv 2611.15835 (11 Nov 2026) [full: abstract, intro, related work, §3.2, discussion; appendices not read].**
   - What it does: anytime-valid upper confidence sequence on the missing mass of unseen failure modes of AI agents, validated on coding-agent traces; cites Böhme 2018; extends to data-adaptive task sampling by inverse propensity weighting provided the propensity ratio to the target distribution is known and bounded; extends to imperfect LLM failure labels; notes that everything depends on how "unseen" is defined (including a mitigation-based definition, which is close to M1's patch-equivalence idea).
   - Delta: no adversary; the estimand is missing mass under a deployment distribution, not the size of a breakable set; no cross-campaign overlap; an attacker's propensities are unknown, so the IPW extension does not cover red teaming. Future work lists multi-model bandits and agent swarms, not attackers.
   - Does the delta matter? Yes, and it is the delta the paper must defend first: this is nine days old, from a group able to do the adversarial extension, and it already owns "Good-Turing for AI-agent failure modes with a guarantee" and "adaptive sampling handled". The cards' reading of it is accurate.

3. **Capture–recapture for defect content from independent finders: Briand, El Emam, Freimut, Laitenberger, ISERN-97-22 / ISSRE 1997 [full, tech-report version], journal version IEEE TSE 2000, doi 10.1109/32.852741 [index]; Petersson, Thelin, Runeson, Wohlin, JSS 72(2) 2004, doi 10.1016/S0164-1212(03)00090-6 [index]; Scott & Wohlin, "Capture-recapture in Software Unit Testing", ESEM 2008 [full]. And its security transplant: Geer, "For Good Measure: The Undiscovered", ;login: 40(2), April 2015 [full].**
   - What it does: an incidence matrix of independent inspectors/testers by defects; models M0, Mt, Mh, Mth with jackknife and Chao estimators to estimate how many defects nobody found; heterogeneous detectability (Mh) is the dominant effect; unreliable below 4 finders, with systematic underestimation. Geer proposes exactly "three or more independent penetration tests against the same target" (or several static-analysis firms) with capture–recapture to estimate latent vulnerabilities, and lists the objections himself: unequal catchability of flaws and finders, non-static population, marking changes behaviour, independence.
   - Delta: S3's design (independent replicate campaigns as sampling units, scenario-specific detection probabilities, incidence-based lower bound) is model Mh with a Chao-type bound, applied to red-team campaigns against an AI system instead of inspectors or pen-test teams. With scenario-as-species the population even has a known ceiling (the benchmark size), which is the classical closed-population case.
   - Does the delta matter? S3's "key insight" (overlap between independent campaigns speaks about the unseen) is not new as an idea in security; Geer states it for pen tests, although as a proposal without data. What remains is doing it for LLM agents and testing it against a held-out stronger attacker. A security referee is likely to know the Geer/Schneier discussion; an ICML referee may not, but the paper should cite it or risk a "known since 2015" review.

4. **Feng et al., "Statistical Estimation of Adversarial Risk in LLMs under Best-of-N Sampling" (SABER), arXiv 2601.22636 [full, main text + Appendix B.1]; with Hughes et al., "Best-of-N Jailbreaking", arXiv 2412.03556 [abs].**
   - What it does: Beta mixture over per-query success probability, Beta-binomial MLE from a small budget, analytic law for ASR@N, confidence intervals; shows attacker rankings reverse between ASR@1 and ASR@15. Appendix B.1.2 adds a spike-and-slab "unbreakable-sample" extension in which ASR@N saturates at 1 − π, i.e. the breakable fraction appears as an explicit parameter.
   - Delta: i.i.d. resampling of one attacker pipeline, parametric, no class structure, no cross-attacker overlap, no adaptive attackers, chat jailbreaks not agents; I did not see π being estimated or validated in the pages read.
   - Does the delta matter? Partly. A Beta-binomial over per-scenario detection probability is statistically the abundance version of a heterogeneity capture–recapture model, so SABER-with-π already targets S3's estimand for one attacker. S3's C2 (bound beats "a fitted saturation curve") and M1's C1 must beat this baseline, and the rank-reversal claims (S3-C3, M1-C2) are not new as a phenomenon: SABER reports one, and arXiv 2610.26595 [abs] reports that changing the number and arrangement of attempts reverses a ranking.

5. **Unseen-species estimators already used in LLM/ML evaluation outside security.**
   - Li, Xin, Long, Su, "Evaluating the Unseen Capabilities: How Many Theorems Do LLMs Know?" (KnowSum), arXiv 2506.02058 [abs]: extrapolates unseen knowledge from frequency counts and shows model rankings change once the unseen part is counted.
   - Pal et al., "Blind-Spot Mass", arXiv 2604.05057 [abs]: Good-Turing for deployment coverage.
   - Lee & Böhme, "How Much is Unseen Depends Chiefly on Information About the Seen", ICLR 2025, arXiv 2402.05835 [abs]: improved missing-mass estimators.
   - Chung et al., "Estimating the Impact of Unknown Unknowns on Aggregate Query Results", arXiv 1507.05591 [abs] (overlap between data sources estimates what is missing) and "A Data Quality Metric (DQM)", arXiv 1611.04878 [abs] (species estimators for undetected errors across crowd workers; standard estimators reported unstable under false positives).
   - Hernández-Suárez & Hernández-Cuevas, arXiv 2607.17061 [full]: s/n as an upper bound on the probability a user meets an undiscovered defect; explicitly contrasts the sampler-relative meaning of abundance in fuzzing with a user population.
   - Delta: none involves an adversary or red-team campaigns.
   - Does the delta matter? These remove any claim that bringing unseen-species statistics into ML evaluation is itself the contribution; they do not touch the red-teaming application.

6. **Near neighbours in LLM red-teaming evaluation that stop short of estimating the unseen** (all [abs] unless noted): Maple et al., arXiv 2605.09070, "Union Coverage" across attack configurations, which implicitly sets the undiscovered part to zero (S3's baseline 3 already exists under a name); Topol, arXiv 2605.12869, survival analysis of time-to-jailbreak; Kapoor et al., arXiv 2610.22754, harmful reachability; Tava, arXiv 2611.19680, diversity retention; Pathade et al., arXiv 2609.25173, ASR as a family of metrics; competition papers arXiv 2603.15714, 2507.20526, 2506.09956, which report transfer and universal attacks descriptively; Feffer et al., arXiv 2401.15897 [full, selected pages], which asks "what are the risks the red-teaming activity potentially missed?" as a reporting question with no method; Jones et al., arXiv 2502.16797, extreme-value forecasting of rare behaviours.
   - Delta: none estimates what was not found from frequency or overlap structure.
   - Does it matter? They show the problem is recognised and the gap is real, and they are the baselines a referee will ask for.

## Parts of the idea already anticipated (be specific, claim by claim)

- **"A red-team campaign is a species-discovery process"** (R1 merged core; M1 key insight): anticipated as a framing for security testing by STADS (2018), which names residual risk, discovery probability, richness, coverage and extrapolation, and by 2611.15835 for AI-agent failure modes. New only in the object (LLM/agent red teaming).
- **"Report discovery probability instead of 'ASR = 0' when nothing is found"** (M1 approach 3): this is STADS's residual-risk statement and the exact subject of Böhme et al. 2021; 2611.15835 gives the anytime-valid version for AI systems.
- **"Adaptive attackers bias the estimators; import the fuzzing correction"** (M1): the bias and its correction are Böhme et al. 2021; the idea contributes the import, and M1 itself flags that the corrections may not hold for LLM attackers. STADS also says the framework does not apply to search that avoids repeats, which describes attackers that deduplicate or keep an archive (quality-diversity, strategy libraries).
- **"Overlap between independent campaigns gives a lower bound on what is undiscovered"** (S3 key insight, approach 2–3): anticipated in software inspections (Briand et al.; Petersson et al.) and in unit testing (Scott & Wohlin), and proposed verbatim for independent penetration tests by Geer (2015). Chung et al. (2015) use the same overlap argument for missing data. The lower-bound property under heterogeneous detection probabilities is the standard property of Chao-type estimators (Gotelli & Colwell chapter [full]: all the non-parametric estimators should be read as lower bounds).
- **"Exchangeable replicates with scenario-specific detection probabilities"** (S3 approach 4): this is model Mh. Briand et al. already found Mh to be the model that matters and that fewer than 4 independent finders is unreliable, which bears directly on how many attacker configurations S3 needs.
- **"Rarefaction/extrapolation puts unequal budgets on one axis; compare at equal coverage"** (S3, M1 approach 4): standard ecology practice and part of STADS (extrapolation, effort needed for a target coverage). Gotelli & Colwell warn that rarefaction should not be used to compare samples collected by different methods, which is precisely the attacker-comparison use; the paper would need to answer that.
- **"A low discovery rate under attacker A says nothing about attacker B"** (S3-C4, first half): stated in STADS (claims hold with respect to that fuzzer) and in 2607.17061 (abundance is an artefact of the sampler).
- **"Breakable fraction" as the estimand** (S3): appears as 1 − π in SABER's unbreakable-sample extension for one attacker under i.i.d. resampling.
- **"Rankings by ASR@k disagree with rankings that account for budget or the unseen"** (S3-C3, M1-C2): rank reversal across budgets is reported by SABER and by 2610.26595; ranking change after unseen-species correction is reported by KnowSum for LLM knowledge. The specific claim that RL-trained attackers rank lower by richness is not anticipated, although 2611.19680 documents the diversity loss that predicts it.
- **Standard objections, and who has answered them** (key question 2):
  - Unequal catchability / non-exchangeable classes: raised by Geer; answered in inspections by Mh models and by treating estimates as lower bounds.
  - Adaptive sampler: raised and partly answered for greybox fuzzing (Böhme et al. 2021); answered for known propensities by IPW (2611.15835); a 2026 registered report on coverage-feedback effects on reachability estimators exists (Attanayake et al., Fuzzing Workshop 2026, doi 10.14722/fuzzing.2026.23010 [index]) as does a reliability assessment of the estimators (arXiv 2507.17093 [abs], no results in the abstract). Not answered for learning attackers with cross-run memory or for repeat-avoiding search.
  - Open or unbounded class space: Spring, arXiv 2304.09259 [abs], argues undiscovered vulnerabilities are effectively unlimited, which makes richness ill-posed and favours missing-mass estimands; Gotelli & Colwell list the closed-assemblage assumption.
  - Arbitrary class definition: 2611.15835 names it as its main limitation; Deng, Umbach, Neufeld, ISME J 2024, doi 10.1093/ismejo/wrae106 [index, title only] is a published warning that changing how units are clustered invalidates Chao1/ACE; DQM reports instability under false positives. Nobody found has validated a class definition by a mitigation-equivalence test.
  - Too few, uneven or non-independent finders: Briand et al. (fewer than 4 unreliable); Scott & Wohlin (no overlap, no estimate); Kansal, "How Many Label Errors Are Left?", Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.23205993 / GitHub (Oct 2026) [full README]: a pre-registered negative result in which capture–recapture over 3–4 LLM judges was calibrated but too wide to beat a plain count. This is the closest evidence on how the method behaves with a handful of LLM-based "finders", and it is discouraging for S3's informativeness claim (C2).
  - Independence of finders: Ozment (WEIS 2005 [full]) and Herr–Schneier–Morris (2017 [full]) both flag that independent rediscovery is hard to establish and neither turns rediscovery rates into a pool-size estimate; Herr et al. mention density estimation only as future work.

## Parts with no prior work found

- Any use of overlap between independent red-team campaigns, or of singleton/doubleton counts in attack logs, to estimate undiscovered vulnerabilities of an ML model, LLM or LLM agent (key question 1). Zero hits on every boolean arXiv query combining the estimator vocabulary with red teaming / jailbreak / prompt injection; nothing in the alphaXiv, web, Crossref or the two OpenAlex searches. This is absence of evidence under the limits listed below, not proof.
- Empirical validation of such an estimate against a held-out stronger attacker family or a much larger pooled campaign (S3-C1, M1-C3). The fuzzing literature validates against longer runs of the same tool; no one found validates against a different, stronger adversary.
- A victim-side, state-verified species definition for agent attacks with a patch-equivalence check that two attacks are "the same vulnerability" (M1 approach 1c). 2611.15835 mentions a mitigation-based notion of "unseen" in one sentence but does not build or test it.
- Evidence on whether the fuzzing adaptive-bias estimators hold for LLM/RL attackers (M1-C1), and the specific claim that RL attackers rank lower by richness than by ASR.
- The transfer-as-sampling-artefact claim (M1-C4): unseen-shared-species correction for cross-defender attack overlap. Shared-species estimators exist in ecology (not opened this session); no application to attack transfer was found.
- Guidance documents: the UK AISI safeguard-evaluation principles page, the Anthropic abstractive red-teaming post and the Feffer et al. survey contain no statistical method for what red teaming missed. NIST AI RMF / AI 100-2, METR and vendor system cards were not opened, so this is a partial negative.

## Verdict: novel | incremental | anticipated — reason

**Incremental.**

No work was found that applies unseen-species or capture–recapture estimation to red-team campaigns against ML/LLM systems, so the idea is not anticipated as an application. Every methodological component is anticipated in adjacent fields: the species framing, residual risk, extrapolation and adaptive-bias correction in fuzzing (Böhme 2018, 2021); overlap between independent finders with heterogeneous detectability in software inspections, and proposed for independent penetration tests by Geer in 2015; missing-mass guarantees for AI-agent failure modes, including a form of adaptive sampling, in arXiv 2611.15835; the breakable-fraction estimand and budget rank reversals in SABER. Answering key question 3: as specified, the method contribution is "apply Chao / Good-Turing / Mh to red-team logs", and the "statistical guarantee" in R1's contribution line would rest on textbook lower-bound properties. What is more than that is empirical: the held-out-attacker validation, the patch-equivalence validation of species, and the test of whether fuzzing's bias corrections survive LLM attackers. That fits ICML's evaluation area as a measurement-validation study (or M1's negative-result fallback), but a referee who knows STADS, the Geer proposal or 2611.15835 will not accept a "new method" framing. The existing evidence on few and uneven finders (Briand et al.; Scott & Wohlin; the 2026 LLM-judge negative result) also suggests the bound may be valid but too loose with 5 attacker families, which is the outcome both cards list as fatal.
