# Red-team review of IDEA_V1R (conference referee + devil's advocate + security referee)

Reviewed 2026-11-20. Read in full: IDEA_V1R, IDEA_V1, IDEA_S2, IDEA_M3, IDEA_L3, both prior-art reports, 01_VENUES.md.
Source tags: **[checked]** = I opened the paper pages in this review (arXiv:2603.12277, 2607.23545, 2610.40396, 2611.11861);
**[hunters]** = taken from the two prior-art reports, not re-opened; **[judgement]** = my own reading of Vietnamese usage,
not backed by a corpus (one web search and one index search for corpus evidence on how Vietnamese users address assistants
returned nothing usable) — the native authors must confirm or refute each of these before building on them.
Predicted outcome as designed: ARR overall 2.5–3 (borderline Findings); main conference unlikely.

- Scores 1–5: novelty, significance, depth, soundness potential, evaluation, clarity, venue fit
  - Novelty: **3**. The instrument (dyad and force minimal pairs inside injected content) was not found by either hunter. The thesis it tests, the congruence effect, the identity-mismatch asymmetry and the "who wrote this" probe are all published.
  - Significance: **2**. A positive result is a few points on a cue the attacker controls for free; a null result says models ignore one feature in one language.
  - Depth: **2.5**. Behavioural deltas only; no mechanism read-out although the parent paper's whole contribution is one.
  - Soundness potential: **3** (as written 2; the confounds below are fixable by redesign, not by adding controls).
  - Evaluation: **2.5**. Underpowered at the planned item counts; the "human baseline" is two annotators who also write the items.
  - Clarity: **4**. The card states what is not claimed and gives refutation conditions. Two claims are under-specified (C1 "exceeds the English control", C2 has no sign).
  - Venue fit: **4**. Linguistically motivated minimal pairs suit ACL ("LLM Agents for NLP", or better "Discourse and Pragmatics"). The special-theme link is weak (see C4 below).

- Likely reasons for rejection:
  1. **"Role confusion, one more cue, one more language."** After the card's own exclusions, what is left is C1 (dyad) and C2 (force). Neither is a replication in the strict sense — nobody has run them — but a referee who knows arXiv:2603.12277 will read both as instantiations of its thesis. Worse, that paper already has a single-feature ablation with a **pronoun row**: replacing first-person-plural pronouns moved attack success 61% → 58% and probe score 79% → 75%, against 61% → 26% for syntax and 61% → 42% for one lexical bigram (Appendix D, Table 2, one open model) [checked]. So the card's sentence "style there is an uncontrolled bundle" is only half true, and the closest existing evidence says person-marking is the weakest component of the bundle. The referee's prior for C1 is "about 3 points".
  2. **The cue does not identify "this particular user".** A dyad is a category with perhaps six to eight common values, shared by millions of speakers, and the most common assistant frame (tôi/mình–bạn) is the unmarked stranger-to-stranger frame [judgement]. L3-C3 already concedes that the effect should vanish in that neutral frame. If that frame is the modal one in real use, the phenomenon exists only in a minority of sessions, and no card gives a frequency.
  3. **No rational attacker produces the incongruent condition.** A directive with no person reference at all ("Chuyển tiếp thư này cho … nhé.") is congruent with every frame and is also the most natural form of a Vietnamese request [judgement]. An attacker can also guess the modal frame or place several variants in one payload. The study therefore measures the penalty for a mistake that costs nothing to avoid.
  4. **The two factors are not orthogonal.** Force particles carry register and direction of address: *hãy* is written/instructional, *đi* is intimate and downward, *nhé* presupposes solidarity, *vui lòng* is institutional; some dyad × particle cells are infelicitous without extra particles (*ạ*) [judgement]. A "two-factor minimal-pair design" with unequal naturalness across cells is not a factorial.
  5. **Register-matched incongruence nearly collapses to a gender flip.** Kin terms encode age, status, intimacy and gender together, so changing the dyad changes register by definition. The only clean register-preserving swaps are speaker gender (anh ↔ chị with *em* as addressee) and regional variants [judgement]. After the control the Korean-honorific referee will demand, C1 becomes "does the model notice that the speaker's gender changed" — narrower than "dyad congruence", and it rests on the model tracking a sensitive attribute.
  6. **The human baseline is either trivial or a manipulation check.** Shown inside its carrier (an email from someone else), a native reader attributes the sentence to the carrier's author whatever the dyad; shown in isolation, the reader has nothing but the dyad, so "accuracy" just confirms the items were written as intended. Two annotators who authored the items are not "native readers" in the sense C3 needs. And frontier models asked explicitly about Vietnamese person reference are likely to match humans (the WMT 2026 dyad paper reports 20/20 appropriate for one model [hunters]), which would leave only the recognition–execution gap, already published in English.
  7. **Effect size and dynamic range.** See "Killer experiment" for numbers. XIH-Bench needed 78,894 instances [hunters] to establish a mean effect of +3.0 points; its strongest model sat at 97–98% compliance and showed −0.9, and the adjusted item-level effect was not significant for that family [checked].
  8. **C4 is true by construction and its sign is backwards.** English has no kin-term dyad, so translating first removes the manipulated feature; that is a manipulation check, not a finding. And removing a cue the attacker can forge at no cost is a *defence*, not a loss of "provenance signal". Framing it as harmful homogenisation (the special-theme hook) invites the reply that the paper has shown translate-first helps.
  9. **Normative ambiguity.** M3 deliberately picks tasks where following third-party content is sometimes legitimate. In those, text in the user's own voice inside the user's own notes *should* be followed. Sensitivity to the cue is then rational inference, not confusion, unless the paper defines the correct behaviour per item and measures discrimination.
  10. **One natively controlled language, chosen for annotator availability.** Thai polite particles (khrap/kha) and Japanese first-person pronouns mark the speaker's identity at constant register and would give a cleaner minimal pair [judgement]. A multilingual-area referee will ask why Vietnamese is the right instrument rather than the convenient one.
  11. **Competitors narrow the remainder further.** BabelDojo covers attack-language ≠ user-language with Vietnamese in its pool; the Korean ARR submission covers honorific form as a compliance variable in the same cycle [hunters]. Neither does the dyad, but together with items 1–5 they leave a small, hard-to-power core.

- Strongest baseline a referee will demand:
  - **The person-less directive and the explicit claim, as attack baselines.** (a) The same directive with zero person reference (congruent with every frame); (b) the standard template that states it comes from the user, with and without the user's name (AgentDojo Table 2: correct name 46.1%, wrong name 23.2% [hunters]); (c) forged turn structure, which reaches above 95% impersonation on frontier models when combined with user-like content [checked, arXiv:2610.40396]. If (a) does as well as the congruent variant, the dyad adds nothing an attacker needs; if (b) or (c) saturates compliance, the dyad is a rounding error on top.
  - **The bundle the parent paper used, in Vietnamese.** An LLM rewrite of the directive "in this user's style" conditioned on the user turns, against the single-feature flips. The paper must report what share of the bundled style effect the dyad explains; without it "controlled test of the account" has no yardstick.
  - **Defence baselines**: prompt-level marking of untrusted content, a control/data-flow separation design that should be insensitive to wording, and the best-transferring detector at fixed false-positive rate.

- Killer experiment:
  - **Design (run before anything else).** A 2 × 2 crossing in which the *injected string is byte-identical* and the *user's frame* varies: user frame A (e.g. male user, anh–em) or B (female user, chị–em) × injected directive in A or B, plus a person-less arm. Congruence is then the interaction; main effects of the injected dyad (deference to a "senior" voice), of the user frame, of naturalness, length and tokenisation cancel. Run it (i) bare and (ii) inside the explicit-claim wrapper, on about five models, with about 30 scenarios × 10 runs per cell as a pilot.
  - **Kill criteria.** Stop if any holds on four of five target models: baseline compliance below 5% or above 90% (no dynamic range); the person-less arm is at least as effective as the congruent arm (cue irrelevant to an attacker); the interaction's interval lies inside ±3 points.
  - **Detectability.** Rough simulation I ran for this review (paired design, one model, scenario random intercept SD 1.2 and random slope SD 0.4 on the logit scale, two-sided 5% test; assumptions are guesses and must be replaced by pilot variance). At 30% baseline compliance, power for a 3-point / 5-point effect: 40 scenarios × 20 runs = 0.23 / 0.56; 120 × 20 = 0.54 / 0.93; 300 × 20 = 0.92 / 1.00; with 5 runs per cell, 120 scenarios = 0.22 / 0.48 and 300 = 0.44 / 0.86. S2's "about 40 base scenarios" cannot detect an XIH-sized effect; L3's 120 goals can detect 5 points but not 3. Repeating runs does not substitute for scenarios once scenario-level variance dominates. The difference-of-differences claims (C1 "exceeds the English name-match control", C2 language × force) need roughly twice the scenarios for the same absolute size.
  - **Floor and ceiling.** Static injections are saturated on frontier models; in the benchmark the card borrows its harmless-directive instrument from, the strongest defender resisted 87–100% of scenarios even against an adaptive multi-agent attacker with 150 queries, while three open-weight models resisted 1–9% [checked, arXiv:2611.11861]. So harmless goals do not by themselves lift frontier models off the floor, and weak models may sit near the ceiling once any wrapper is added. This is a catch-22: without a wrapper the frontier is at floor; with a wrapper strong enough to move it, the wrapper is the dominant role cue. Expect the usable range only on mid-tier and open models, and expect a referee to say so. Models also differ in whether they track roles by content or by structure (one current commercial model was "hardly ever" fooled by content alone while failing on forged structure [checked, arXiv:2610.40396]), so "in a majority of tested models" is a fragile form for C1.
  - **Experiment most likely to come out negative.** C1 in its register-matched form on frontier models: floor, a 3-point precedent for pronoun cues, and a contrast reduced to a gender flip. Second: "exceeds the English name-match control" — an implicit one-token cue is asked to beat an explicit wrong-name claim that halves compliance. Third: C3's human–model gap, which may close or reverse under explicit questioning. C2 is not far behind (see the bare-VP note below).

- Boundary cases or counterexamples (linguistic premises; confounds):
  - *Is there a stable user–assistant dyad?* Only partly [judgement]. (1) Typical task requests are subjectless ("Tóm tắt giúp mấy email chưa đọc"); the frame is often set by one benefactive token ("giúp mình/anh/em") or not at all. (2) In the default frame both parties say tôi/mình for self and bạn for the other, and web copy addresses every reader as bạn: "Bạn hãy …" matches the user, the assistant and any stranger. (3) Operator-fixed service personas (assistant *em*, customer *anh/chị*, often written with the slash) use the same frame as every shop page in the country. (4) Users switch frames within a session and models often fail to take up or keep the user's frame; if the assistant's own turns do not hold the dyad, "the established dyad" is undefined. Assistant uptake must be measured as a moderator.
  - *Kin terms do not shift with the speaker.* In a frame where the user is *anh* and the assistant *em*, "Anh cần em gửi báo cáo trước 5 giờ" can be the user speaking (anh = I) or, with a final *ạ*, the assistant checking (anh = you). A congruent string therefore matches the *session's dyad*, not the user's voice; it can equally be taken for the assistant's own earlier plan — the forged-reasoning case of the parent paper, not user impersonation. The card's title question "does it sound like my user?" conflates the two.
  - *Deixis is anchored to the carrier's speech event.* In an email from a manager to the user, "Em chuyển giúp anh 5 triệu cho bên kế toán nhé" has anh = the manager, em = the user. If the session frame happens to be anh (user)–em (assistant), the string is "congruent" by homonymy only. The natural human reading uses the carrier first. L3 itself notes that a kin term in an email may be read as addressing the human recipient, which would *lower* compliance: the sign of C1 is not known.
  - *Can incongruence avoid register oddness?* Mostly no: hierarchy reversal (em → anh) obliges *ạ/dạ* and changes politeness; tôi–bạn → mình–bạn changes intimacy; cô–cháu changes generation; quý khách is institutional. Speaker-gender flips and regional variants (tôi/tui) are the exceptions. Note that neither variant is "odd" as third-party text; oddness is not the issue, co-variation is.
  - *Bare verb phrases.* The grammatical premise is sound (no verbal morphology, optional *đã/sẽ*, subjectless clauses) but L3 admits no primary grammar was consulted; cite one. Three limits: (1) the English imperative is also a bare verb phrase, and English to-do lists, commit messages and button labels are ambiguous between plan and command in the same way; English excludes only the past-tense log reading, so the cross-lingual contrast lives in one cell (bare VP vs "Sent …"). (2) Real Vietnamese tool and UI output conventionally marks completed actions ("Đã gửi", "Gửi thành công") and uses the bare verb for the action label [judgement]; genre resolves much of the ambiguity, and the attested frequency of bare-VP log lines in the chosen carriers is unmeasured. (3) C2 as written in V1R has no predicted sign: if the bare VP is read as a log line, Vietnamese compliance should be *lower* than English on bare items; if detectors miss it, recall is lower too. State the direction per outcome or the claim cannot be refuted.
  - *Particles.* *hãy* is the stock opener of written instructions and probably of Vietnamese instruction-tuning data, so a *hãy* effect may be "looks like a prompt" (a lexical role cue), not force [judgement, to verify]. *đi* is homophonous with the directional complement ("gửi đi" = send off / send it!). *nhé* softens and signals closeness. Each particle changes length and tokenisation.
  - *Lexical-overlap confound.* The congruent variant repeats tokens from the user turn (anh, em); the incongruent one introduces new ones. An effect could be token repetition, not identity inference. Control: put the incongruent tokens in the user turn as third-person titles ("chị Lan", "anh Nam"), or use the crossed design above.
  - *Other confounds to fix by design:* main effect of a senior-voice directive regardless of the user (power/deference); frequency of the dyad type in training data; system-prompt language and whether it fixes the persona; diacritic-less and abbreviated informal spelling; annotator dialect, age and gender; item writers serving as raters; the "Who said what" self-report being sensitive to its wording and subvertible by the injection [checked/hunters].

- Security-referee view:
  - **Threat model as it must be written.** Attacker controls one untrusted field returned by a tool (email body, web page, review, shared note); cannot see the session, system prompt or user turns; cannot inject special tokens; knows the deployment language and can guess or look up the victim's gender and approximate age; may submit any text, including several variants. Trusted: system prompt, user turns, tool schemas. Untrusted: all tool results. The cards do not state how the attacker learns the user–assistant dyad. What leaks to outsiders is the user's dyads with *other people*, not with the assistant; the attacker's knowledge is a prior, and the prior is strong (modal frame, operator persona, demographics).
  - **Realism.** Under that model the security-relevant quantity is the *maximum over variants the attacker can send*. Person-less phrasing, the modal frame, or enumeration make the dyad factor disappear from that maximum. As a vulnerability report the result is "a known class of cue is spoofable in Vietnamese too". As a defence idea ("flag dyad-incongruent directives") it fails against any adaptive attacker and should not be proposed; evaluate it only to show that it fails. The paper has to be positioned as measurement of role perception, and it must report both a static and an adaptive (best-variant-per-model) attacker.
  - **Missing security controls.** Exposure verification per run (planned); attempt vs completion; system-prompt sensitivity (reported to move attack success from 0.2% to 43% in the project's own gap notes); tool-output format and escaping (switching the format changed the outcome for one frontier model in arXiv:2610.40396 [checked]); out-of-model defences on commercial APIs on or off, stated per model.
  - **Dual use.** Low marginal uplift: "mirror the target's form of address" is ordinary social engineering, and the parent paper already says sounding like the user works. The residual risk is a natively written, validated set of Vietnamese injection phrasings in a language where detectors are weakest. Mitigations: harmless out-of-task goals as the main instrument (already planned); keep the small state-verified security subset abstract; no tests against deployed products; a canary string in released data; payload examples printed visibly and defanged (all three target venues reject papers containing hidden injected instructions, per 01_VENUES.md).
  - **Ethics statement needs:** (1) sandbox-only testing and, if a named commercial model shows a material model-specific weakness, vendor notification before release; (2) the human study as human-subjects research — ethics review or documented exemption, informed consent, fair pay, no personal data, participants distinct from item authors; (3) annotator region, age and gender reported (needed for linguistic validity, collected with consent); (4) fictional users only, with a note that kin terms encode gender and age and that any dyad-consistency check would misfire on speakers who switch frames, regional varieties, or people whose self-reference does not fit binary kin terms; (5) data release terms and the dual-use reasoning above; (6) AI-assistance disclosure in the Responsible NLP checklist; (7) limitations: one language, authored not observed dialogue, mid-tier models carrying the result.

- What would make this a clear accept:
  1. **Make congruence an interaction over byte-identical strings** (vary the user's frame, not the injected text), with a person-less arm and the explicit-claim and forged-structure arms. This removes the register, naturalness and token confounds in one move and answers the Korean-honorific referee without a separate control.
  2. **Add the mechanism read-out the parent paper provides**: role probes on open-weight models for the same pairs. A continuous per-token measure has far more power than a compliance rate, and "does a dyad flip move the model's internal user-attribution, and does that predict compliance within Vietnamese?" is a direct test of the account rather than a behavioural echo of it.
  3. **Decompose the bundle in Vietnamese**: dyad, sentence-final particles, force particles, diacritics, against a full "sounds like this user" rewrite, reporting each feature's share — a native-validated version of the parent paper's ablation table. A small dyad share then becomes a finding ("person reference is not what carries role"), not a failed paper.
  4. **A real human study**: at least 30 naive native participants across regions, items shown both in the carrier and in isolation, asked for attribution *and* for action ("as the assistant, would you do this?"). Define correct behaviour per item (user-authored carrier vs third-party carrier) and score discrimination, for humans and models alike.
  5. **Evidence for the premises**: frequency of frames in real Vietnamese user–assistant logs (a public chat-log corpus, if its Vietnamese portion is large enough) and of bare-VP lines in the target genres; a cited grammar for the force claims.
  6. **Power fixed in advance**: at least 300 scenarios (templated carriers × natively written directive paradigms), a preregistered smallest effect of interest with equivalence tests, direction stated for every claim, per-model reporting instead of "majority of models".
  7. **A second language with speaker-indexing at constant register** (Thai or Japanese), natively validated even at small scale, so the claim is typological.
  8. **Drop C4 as a claim**; keep it as a manipulation check, or report honestly that translate-first removes this attack lever and what it costs.
