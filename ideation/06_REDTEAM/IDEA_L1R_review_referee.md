# Red-team review of IDEA_L1R (conference referee + journal referee)

Reviewed 2026-11-20 against `04_IDEAS/IDEA_L1R.md`, both prior-art reports and `01_VENUES.md`. Sources I opened myself are listed at the end; everything else about prior work rests on the two hunter reports. Vietnamese judgments below are my own readings as a referee and are marked as predictions to be tested with native speakers, not as facts.

- Scores 1–5: novelty, significance, depth, soundness potential, evaluation, clarity, venue fit
  - **Novelty 3.** Layer D (dyad items in a memory-writing benchmark, any Vietnamese memory benchmark) was not found by either hunter and I found nothing in one further search. But the phenomenon is person-reference resolution across a context boundary, which the zero-pronoun and honorific literature has studied for years, and the "flip the dyad, keep the string" manipulation has a Vietnamese precedent (2026.wmt-main.16). Layer T is one step past a dense 2026 cluster.
  - **Significance 2** as specified (would be 4 with evidence that the cases occur in real use). The card never shows that Vietnamese users produce these frames when talking to an assistant, and its headline mechanism is true by construction (see rejection reason 1).
  - **Depth 3.** The idea is sharper than "tools are worse in Vietnamese", but the mechanism stops at "the frame was outside the window".
  - **Soundness potential 3.** Fixable, but today the gold is categorical on phenomena that are graded, two annotators decide it, and two of four claims are confounded.
  - **Evaluation 3.** Good instincts (human floor, family-clustered intervals, framework-native writers, a decisive pilot). Missing the four controls a referee will ask for first (below).
  - **Clarity 3.** Two benchmarks, four claims, a probe and 15–20 systems in eight pages; the key term "holder" is used for something else than what the example manipulates.
  - **Venue fit 4.** Discourse and Pragmatics is the right area; the Multilingualism area is a weaker fit with one language. The special-theme hook through C1b is thin.
  - **Predicted ARR outcome as specified:** overall 2.5–3 with split reviewers; Findings is the realistic ceiling, main conference unlikely.

- Likely reasons for rejection:
  1. **The central result is a tautology.** If the target turn is string-identical and the frame-setting turn is outside what the writer receives, the two members of a pair give the writer the same input, so it must give the same output. "Same-holder rate > 50%" (C1) and "accuracy drops when the frame leaves the window" (C2) then say only that a system cannot use information it was not given, and the dyad register "removing most of the drop" says only that giving the information back helps. A referee will write exactly this. The non-trivial questions are different ones: (a) what does each framework actually pass to its writer, (b) when the frame is missing, does the writer mark the reference as unresolved or silently pick someone, and in which direction, (c) with the frame visible, are models below native speakers.
  2. **The "window" is not a hard edge in at least one target system.** Mem0's own paper documents an extraction call that is fed a periodically refreshed summary of the whole conversation, the most recent m messages (ten in their experiments) and the new message pair. Whether the dyad survives in that summary is an open empirical matter; a step function is not what this architecture predicts. The card's risk 5 notes that windows must be read from code, but the claims are already written as if the answer were known.
  3. **No evidence the frames occur in user–assistant dialogue.** See the boundary-case section on how users address assistants. Without a prevalence estimate from natural or elicited conversations, the benchmark reads as items authored to fail. Worse, keeping the window free of every address term from both user and assistant, which string identity requires, is itself unnatural Vietnamese: in a kin-term dyad both parties repeat the terms almost every turn, so the frame is normally recoverable locally.
  4. **The showcase example does not have a clean two-way reading**, and the card manipulates the wrong dyad (details below). A Vietnamese-speaking reviewer will see this on page 1.
  5. **Categorical gold on graded phenomena.** Six experiments over twenty clause-embedding predicates gave Degen and Tonhauser no empirical basis for a categorical factive class; projection came out graded. Layer T's license/block twins assume the class exists. Two annotators plus an "ambiguous" bin is not the instrument for this; CommitmentBank-style graded judgments from many speakers are.
  6. **Two papers in one.** Layer T is English-centred, crowded and at high scoop risk; Layer D is the protected part. Reviewers will say neither is developed fully in eight pages, and the weaker half will set the score.
  7. **C1b is a straw man.** "Translation must commit to I/you/she before the writer runs" is a failure only if the translator sees less context than the extractor. With the same context, translate-then-extract should match direct extraction except on genuinely ambiguous items, where English cannot keep the ambiguity. That narrower claim is defensible; the stated one is not.
  8. **Vietnamese-specificity is overstated for (c)–(e).** A null subject under a report verb exists in Chinese, Japanese and Korean (the card's own neighbour 2605.31056 covers Chinese); a negatively biased belief verb exists in Mandarin. Only the kin-term person shift is distinctive. A referee will ask why (c)–(e) are "Vietnamese only" and why one language supports a general claim.
  9. **Frontier models may sit at the human floor** on everything except the by-construction cases; the card's own decisive experiment admits this. A paper whose non-trivial half is null goes to Findings at best.
  10. **Falsifiability audit of the claims.**
      - **C1:** numerically falsifiable, but confirmed by construction for windowed writers (reason 1). The 50% threshold is arbitrary. The rate is undefined when a writer stores nothing for one member, so a writer can pass by staying silent; and under admissible-set scoring an identical answer to both members is not an error whenever the two admissible sets overlap. The refutation clause ("best extractor reaches the human floor") is the only real test, and it is likely to fire.
      - **C2:** "refuted if there is no window effect" can only happen if the authors misread the window or the gold does not depend on the frame. Not a test of the hypothesis "locality, not comprehension"; that needs humans given the same truncated input.
      - **C3:** falsifiable, with two defects. Pair accuracy is mechanically below single-item accuracy (two items at 0.9 give about 0.81 if independent), so "≥ 20 points below" must be measured against the product of the members' accuracies, not against single-operator accuracy. And outcomes between 5 and 20 points are neither confirmation nor refutation. Against a lexicon gate the result is guaranteed by how the twins are built; it is informative only for LLM-based cautious extractors.
      - **C4:** not interpretable as stated. For prompted models "held out" means absent from a handful of exemplars, and seen versus unseen combinations differ in intrinsic difficulty. Without rotating which combinations are held out, a gap cannot be attributed to exposure. The card also names "any tuned extractor" but lists none.

- Strongest baseline a referee will demand:
  - **Contextualise-then-extract with the option to abstain**: a Vietnamese-prompted frontier reasoning model that first rewrites each user turn so that every person term is replaced by a named referent or explicitly marked unresolved, using exactly the context the framework passes (for Mem0: summary + recent messages), and only then extracts. This is the standard decontextualisation step (Choi et al. 2021 define it as rewriting a sentence to be interpretable out of context and include an "infeasible" label when it cannot be done) and it is functionally the dyad register. If it closes the gap, the register is not a contribution.
  - **The one-line patch**: add "record how the user and the assistant address each other and how the user refers to named people" to each framework's own summary or profile prompt. Cheaper than the register; must be compared with it.
  - **Information-matched native speakers**: humans given the same truncated input as the writer. Without this, C2 cannot separate locality from comprehension.
  - **Full-history writer as a writer**, not only as a "comprehension reference" (baseline 7): a long-context model that writes memory from the whole session.
  - **Context-matched translation** for C1b: dialogue-level MT given the same context as the extractor, plus turn-level MT as the weak variant.
  - **Language controls**: an English item set with a third-person pronoun whose antecedent lies outside the window (does the same step appear?), and a Chinese null-subject-under-report set. These decide whether the finding is about Vietnamese kin terms or about anaphora across a context boundary.
  - **Vietnamese-adapted models and prompts** (the card claims English person categories in prompts cause harm; that needs a Vietnamese-prompt ablation), and **regional / no-diacritic input conditions** as test conditions rather than metadata. Ten instruction-tuned models lost 2.82% on average under human-written dialect rewrites in VialectBench, with Central varieties worst, so a reviewer has a ready citation for asking.
  - The card's baselines 3–5 (FACTWASH gate, AgentMemGate extractor, hedge-preserving prompt) are right for Layer T and should stay.

- Killer experiment:
  - **An information-matched human-versus-writer comparison on natural material.** Design: reader ∈ {≥ 8 native speakers per item, North and South; strongest extractor; each framework's native writer} × context ∈ {frame visible; frame absent, i.e. exactly what the framework passes} × item source ∈ {authored frame-flip pairs; cases found in real or freely elicited Vietnamese user–assistant conversations, including users who paste family chat threads}. Response options include "cannot tell from this".
  - The idea survives only if at least one of these holds, and the third always: (i) with the frame visible, the writer's choice of referent differs from the human distribution by more than human split-half noise; (ii) with the frame absent, humans mostly answer "cannot tell" while writers commit silently, with a measurable lean toward filing the fact under the user; (iii) the natural-material arm yields such cases at a rate worth a paper (fix the threshold before looking, for example a stated percentage of fact-bearing user turns).
  - If (i) and (ii) both fail, the paper is "information not given cannot be used". If (iii) fails, it is a constructed curiosity. This replaces the card's decisive pilot, which tests only the strongest extractor on authored items.

- Boundary cases or counterexamples (linguistic judgments that may not hold; confounds):
  - **Frame-flip example, "Lan bảo chị nên nghỉ việc để đi học tiếp."**
    - *Which dyad?* The card flips user–Lan seniority. But the term a speaker uses for herself is licensed by her relation to the person she is talking to, here the assistant, not to Lan. If the user addresses the assistant with a neutral pair (mình/tôi – bạn), "chị" can mean the user only as a retained piece of Lan's wording (unmarked direct quotation). The design must state and manipulate two dyads separately: user–assistant and user–Lan.
    - *At least four readings.* "Chị" can be (1) the user herself, (2) the assistant, (3) Lan herself, with "bảo" read as "said", (4) another senior woman, typically the user's elder sister. The syntax is also two-way: "chị" as the person Lan spoke to ("told chị to…") or as subject of the embedded clause ("said that chị should…"). Each context will shift preferences among these, not select one. I expect the "user is em" context to split between (3), (4) and "cannot tell".
    - *The string leaks.* A senior Lan is normally "chị Lan"; bare "Lan" signals a peer or junior. So the supposedly identical string is biased toward the "Lan junior" context and is marked or odd in the other. One pair member is less natural than the other, which is the usual way minimal pairs fail.
    - *World-knowledge shortcut.* Reading (2) is ruled out because an AI has no job to quit; a model can discard it without using the frame at all.
    - *"Holder" is the wrong word.* The source of the advice is Lan in both contexts and the user's commitment is "attributed only" in both. What flips is who the embedded clause is about. That is reference resolution; calling it holder resolution invites the objection that the commitment machinery of the gold tuple is not what the item tests. It is also unclear that the *write decision* flips, since that depends on a policy about storing third-party facts.
  - **Null subject, "Mẹ bảo về quê ăn Tết."** The directive reading (mother told me/us to come home) is strongly preferred; the reading in which the mother herself is travelling exists but needs support ("sẽ", a repeated "mẹ"). High agreement will reflect the default, not resolved ambiguity. "Bảo" is Northern; Southern "kêu/biểu" are directive only and "nói" leans assertive, so the item's ambiguity profile changes by region rather than merely having "variants". "Mẹ" may be a mother-in-law, or a wife referred to from the children's point of view, so "user's mother" in the record can itself be wrong.
  - **Borrowed perspective breaks the dyad register.** Vietnamese speakers routinely take a third person's viewpoint when choosing a kin term (a woman calling her husband's younger brother "chú" as her child would). A register that stores "speaker self-term, addressee term, third parties with their terms" has no slot for whose viewpoint a term is computed from. Speakers also switch terms mid-conversation to signal mood; Lê Viết Dũng's 2013 survey of human–human address reports such switches within families. A single "current frame" is an idealisation.
  - **biết / nghĩ / tưởng.**
    - *biết:* factive with a declarative complement, but colloquial "không biết + clause" is very often an embedded question with no overt marker or with "chưa / có … không", which carries no presupposition; "biết đâu" means "maybe".
    - *nghĩ:* with a first-person subject it is a hedged self-commitment, not a non-commitment. "Không nghĩ là…" has a surprise use whose complement is true ("Em không nghĩ là mình lại nghỉ việc thật"), so the block twin can license the fact. English "didn't think I'd quit" behaves the same way.
    - *tưởng:* the "not p" inference is a strong default tied to the time of the belief, not an entailment ("cứ tưởng … ai dè", second-person questions, past beliefs later overtaken). Glass's 2025 squib separates a strong contrafactive, for which no verb has been demonstrated, from a weak type that only needs not-p to be compatible with the common ground, where she places Mandarin yǐwéi; "tưởng" looks like the same weak type and the paper must engage that literature. A rule table that outputs "committed-not-p" is too strong.
    - English has no single-word counterpart of "tưởng", so the EN and VI versions of the ATT operator are not parallel.
  - **"Em có nghỉ việc đâu."** The reading claimed (emphatic denial, no "không") is right. Concerns: it is very frequent, so I expect ceiling performance; it denies something salient in the immediately preceding context, which makes it an update/correction item already covered by existing benchmarks and puts the trigger inside any window; "đâu" is also "where" and can stack ("có đi đâu đâu"); Southern "đâu có", Central "mô"; typed without diacritics ("em co nghi viec dau") it becomes ambiguous. Rhetorical forms are more diagnostic: "Ai bảo em nghỉ việc?", "Nghỉ việc gì mà nghỉ", "Làm gì có chuyện em nghỉ việc", and "chưa" versus "không".
  - **How Vietnamese users address an assistant.** Two searches (Vietnamese and English) returned only general usage surveys and studies of human–human address; I found no empirical source, so the card's premise is unmeasured. My expectation, to be tested: neutral pairs (mình/tôi – bạn) and pronoun-less requests are common; some users take the junior role (em, with the assistant as anh/chị); some take the senior role toward assistants with a junior persona; playful mày–tao occurs; assistants mirror the user. Consequences: under neutral pairs the self/addressee flip cannot arise and only third-party kin terms and retained quotations remain; under kin pairs the frame is restated almost every turn. The realistic long-distance case is cross-session: the address pattern is set in an earlier session or in custom instructions and the writer in a later session does not receive it. That, and pasted family chats, are where natural items should come from. Public logs may be thin: Vietnamese is absent from the ten most frequent languages plotted for WildChat.
  - **Confounds.**
    - *User-centric prompt prior:* writers are told to extract facts about the user (hunter B read this in Mem0's prompts), so a lean toward the user is a prompt effect that would also appear in English.
    - *Distance manipulation:* moving the frame turn changes dialogue length, recency and intervening material; needs filler-matched controls.
    - *Assistant turns:* whoever writes them can leak or mirror the frame.
    - *Weak open model:* low Vietnamese competence will be mistaken for locality.
    - *Sampling noise:* same-holder rate needs fixed decoding or repeated runs.
    - *Names:* gender and bare-name versus titled-name cues leak seniority.

- Journal-referee view (Computational Linguistics):
  - A CL referee will judge the linguistics first and the system tables last, because model results date quickly. As a benchmark paper with more tables this is a reject; as a study of discourse-anchored person reference in decontextualising systems, with a resource, it is a plausible major revision.
  - **What the journal version must add.** (1) An explicit model of the phenomenon: person terms whose value depends on an anchor (whose viewpoint) and a dyad, how the anchor shifts under report and quotation, and what a context-free record must contain to stay interpretable. (2) The annotation manual and a disagreement analysis as a primary contribution: many raters per item, graded judgments, regional stratification, an account of which disagreements are ambiguity and which are noise. (3) The gold tuple mapped onto factuality profiles with nested sources (De Facto / FactBank, published in this journal in 2012, read by hunter B); CL referees will expect the lineage to be explicit. (4) A typological argument rather than one language: at least one more language with open-class or kin-based person reference (Thai, Japanese, Korean) and a contrast language that has null subjects but closed-class pronouns (Chinese), so that "kin-term person shift" is separated from "pro-drop". The card lists a third language as optional; for CL it is required. (5) Natural data and the downstream consequence (a wrong referent producing a wrong later answer).
  - **Structure.** Layer T either becomes a separate article or is unified with Layer D under one question ("who is committed to what about whom"); stapled together they will be criticised as two short papers.
  - **Process.** The extension must contain substantial new material beyond the conference paper; the exact requirement was not checked in this session and should be read from the journal's guidelines. `01_VENUES.md` already notes TACL is closed to this route.

- What would make this a clear accept:
  1. **Replace the by-construction claim with the three non-trivial ones** from rejection reason 1: an audited, versioned account of what each framework passes to its writer and whether the address pattern survives; silent commitment versus marking the reference unresolved, with the direction of the bias; and model-versus-human reading distributions when the frame is visible. Run the killer experiment and report it whichever way it falls.
  2. **Ground the items in real use.** A prevalence estimate and a natural test subset (elicited free conversations with a memory-enabled assistant, cross-session address patterns, pasted family chats). Authored minimal pairs become the diagnostic set, not the headline.
  3. **Repair the linguistics before authoring at scale.** Two dyads specified per item; the four-way reading inventory; ≥ 8–10 native raters per item with a "cannot tell" option and North/South balance; naturalness-matched near-minimal pairs alongside string-identical ones; "holder" renamed to what it is (referent of the reported proposition) and kept distinct from source and commitment.
  4. **Make it one paper.** Layer D plus only those Vietnamese scope items that English cannot host (tưởng, the surprise use of "không nghĩ là", rhetorical negation). Move the English twins to a second submission.
  5. **Add the demanded baselines and controls** (contextualise-then-extract with abstention, one-line summary patch, information-matched humans, context-matched MT, English out-of-window pronoun control, Chinese null-subject control, Vietnamese prompts, dialect and no-diacritic conditions).
  6. **Restate the claims so each can fail**: C1 scored jointly with omission rate and against the human reading distribution; C2 as a difference between humans and writers given identical input; C3 against the product of member accuracies with one threshold; C4 with rotated held-out combinations or dropped.
  7. **Show the harm**: a wrong referent stored at write time leading to a wrong later answer or action, moved from "secondary" to a core result.
