# Red-team review of IDEA_F1R (conference referee + security referee + journal referee)

- Scores 1–5: novelty 3, significance 3, depth 2, soundness potential 3, evaluation 2, clarity 3, venue fit 3
  - Predicted outcome at ICML 2027 for a paper that follows the card as written: it clears the area-chair first stage (the topic is in scope) and then lands at weak reject to borderline. The cross-task relation is a real idea; the statistics built around it do not yet support the title.
  - Soundness potential is 2 as written and about 4 if the design change under "What would make this a clear accept" is adopted.
  - Basis. Opened this session: the idea card, both prior-art reports, the venue profile; AndroidWorld `registry.py`, `task_evals/common_validators/sqlite_validators.py`, `task_evals/single/expense.py`; the pages the PDF reader returned for arXiv 2610.09142v3, 2605.10448v1 and 2607.28367v1; the ICML 2027 Contribution Types page (through a fetch summary, not raw HTML). Two further alphaXiv searches for a scoop of the cross-task relation returned 28 records, none closer than those in the reports (titles and abstract snippets only; UndoBench 2610.05622 and AndroidFlux 2610.34384 are thematically nearest and treat undo/recovery as an agent capability, not as a checker audit). Everything else is taken from the two hunter reports and was not re-opened.
  - Corrections to the card found while checking.
    - arXiv 2605.10448 audited 41 AndroidWorld case units with two models, so 82 records, of which 41 were undecidable; its two AndroidWorld "conflicts" are the same recipe task seen once per model. The card's "half of 41 sampled records" should read "half of 82 records from 41 tasks".
    - arXiv 2607.28367: 15.3% is all wrong FAIL verdicts, made of 10.7% evaluator false negatives plus 4.7% broken tasks. Its sample is web, enterprise and desktop only, so it gives no specificity figure for any mobile benchmark.
    - AndroidWorld's generic SQLite add/delete validators already compare the app table before and after and reject any other added, removed or altered row; the expense tasks use them (recipe and calendar task files were not opened). The natural inverse pairs sit largely on these checkers.

- Likely reasons for rejection:
  1. **The headline quantity is not the quantity identified.** The card says the violation rate among compositions where both legs passed lower-bounds P(wrong or extra effect | pass). It lower-bounds something narrower: the probability that at least one of two legs deviates, in a composed population the authors built, on the subset of tasks that have inverses. Moving from there to "wrong PASS verdicts in AndroidWorld" needs three further steps, none free (details under boundary cases). With about 15–20% of templates applicable and a factor 2 for the two legs, a 3% composition-level rate certifies roughly 0.2–0.3% of suite-level passes. A referee who does this arithmetic will say the title oversells.
  2. **The composed benchmark is not the shipped benchmark.** In AndroidWorld's SQLite-backed tasks, `initialize_task` clears the app database and inserts its own target and noise rows, and the delete checker's targets are the rows it inserted itself. A second leg that undoes what the agent just did therefore needs a derived task class and a re-parameterised checker. The paper then audits its own harness, and a benchmark author will say so.
  3. **Coverage is small and unrepresentative.** From the registered task list I count, by task name only, about 8–11 natural inverse pairs (expense add/delete single and multiple, recipe add/delete single and multiple, calendar add/delete one event, Markor create/delete note, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on/off, file and note moves with swapped arguments), touching about 20 of 116 templates. SMS, camera, audio, drawing and the information-retrieval tasks have no inverse. Both AndroidWorld checker defects on record in the artifacts fall outside the pairs: issue #380 (list answers) is an information-retrieval checker, and the target-set bug in arXiv 2605.10448 is in a delete-by-condition recipe task with no add counterpart. The applicable set is where shipped checkers are strongest.
  4. **A simpler baseline dominates.** Step 2 already builds a scripted reference policy for every task and a measured noise mask. Diffing the agent's end state against the scripted run's end state from the same seed uses the same two ingredients, covers every task, needs no composition and no conditioning, and also catches wrong requested effects that a round trip cancels. It is not in the baseline list. Separately, AndroidWorld is small: 116 tasks × 3–5 seeds at a 60–90% pass rate is about 200–500 passes per agent, so a full human audit of every pass is feasible and removes the need for any indirect estimator.
  5. **Thresholds are mutually inconsistent and the claims are underpowered.** The instrument is allowed a 2% false-alarm rate on gold runs; C1 asks for a lower bound above 3% and the negative result asks for an upper bound below 1%. An observed 3% with 2% tolerated false alarms is about 1% net, and a 1% ceiling cannot be reached by an instrument allowed to alarm 2% of the time. C2 (one pair of agents swaps order) is nearly certain by seed noise among near-tied agents. C3 (Spearman ρ < 0.4) over 12–15 templates has a 95% interval of roughly −0.2 to 0.8 around 0.4, so it cannot separate weak from strong correlation.
  6. **The seeding arm (step 5, C4) is not coherent** for deterministic checkers and C4 can "succeed" through imprecision alone (see boundary cases).
  7. **Construct ambiguity.** "Wrong or extra effect" merges two things: the goal was not achieved yet the checker passed, and the goal was achieved with a side effect the benchmark never promised to check. arXiv 2605.10448 keeps the benchmark's own claim apart from a stricter layer that includes preservation of unrelated state. A round trip is informative mainly about the second. That finding is already published for tool-use agents (SABench and neighbours, per the reports), so novelty rests on the instrument alone, in a field with high scoop risk.
  8. **Venue perception.** One Android benchmark, emulator instrumentation, two annotators and a lineage from ISSTA/OOPSLA app testing read as benchmark maintenance. See the last bullet of this list.
  - **Would ICML see this as an ISSTA/ICSE or NeurIPS track paper?** Formally no: the Contribution Types page, as summarised by the fetch tool, lists identifying limitations and failure modes of widely used benchmarks under "Datasets, Evaluations and Benchmarks" and tells reviewers to judge by the selected type. Practically, partly yes. An ICML Evaluation-area reviewer will ask what transfers beyond Android and what the statistical contribution is once the card disclaims the estimator algebra. ISSTA/ICSE/ASE would value the oracle-assessment lineage and accept a single ecosystem; the NeurIPS Evaluations & Datasets track is the natural home if the artefact (observer, relations, patched checkers) is the deliverable. For ICML the paper must be a measurement-method paper with an identification result and more than one benchmark family. The "Negative Results" fallback is weak: a lower-bound instrument cannot show that wrong passes are absent, so a near-zero violation rate says little.

- Strongest baseline a referee will demand:
  - **Gold-differential whole-state oracle**: for every task, diff the agent's end state against the scripted reference run's end state from the same initial state, with the same observer and the same mask. This is the whole-state frame check that arXiv 2610.09142 names as future work, built from parts the proposal already has.
  - **Census audit**: two blind annotators adjudicate every checker-passed run of each agent with retained post-state. At AndroidWorld's size this is the reference standard, not a sample.
  - Then the card's own baselines 1, 3 and 4 (state-reading LLM verifier on every pass; gold-run mutation score; prediction-powered correction from a small audited subset), and a two-inspector capture–recapture estimate using the programmatic checker and the LLM verifier as the two inspectors, which hunter B notes as an unsampled alternative needing no seeding.
  - The relation instrument has one regime where it should beat the gold differential, and the paper must show it: tasks with several valid end states. A gold diff raises false alarms whenever the agent picks a different valid end state; a round trip does not care which valid state leg 1 chose as long as the inverse undoes it.

- Killer experiment:
  - One table on full AndroidWorld, at least three agents including one weak one, at least three seeds. Every pass is adjudicated blind (census), with planted wrong and planted clean cases mixed into the annotators' queue to measure the annotators' own recall and false-alarm rate. Against that reference, report recall, precision and human minutes for: (a) R1–R3 as in the card, (b) agent leg followed by a scripted inverse, (c) gold-differential, (d) LLM verifier, (e) the seed-calibrated prediction.
  - Kill criteria fixed in advance. The idea survives only if the relations either find adjudicated non-benign violations that (c) and (d) miss, or reach the same recall at a clear fraction of the labels. It dies if the mask's false-alarm rate measured on adjudicated-clean *agent* runs (not scripted runs) is of the same order as the violation rate.
  - Scale of the card's "decisive experiment" is too small to decide its own criterion: 16 confirmed of 20 flagged gives a 95% interval of 56–94% for precision, which cannot establish "≥ 0.8".

- Boundary cases or counterexamples (statistical identification; where the lower bound fails):
  - **What is identified.** Let X = 1 when the masked diff between start state and post-round-trip state is non-empty, and E_i = 1 when leg i did something other than "the requested effect and nothing else". The quantity estimated is θ = P(X = 1 | both legs pass, pair in the applicable set, agent). The logical step X = 1 ⇒ E_1 or E_2 holds under three assumptions: (A1) a correct, clean T followed by a correct, clean T⁻¹ restores every unmasked field by *any* admissible route; (A2) the mask covers all benign residue any correct agent route can leave; (A3) taking snapshots does not change state. Under A1–A3, θ ≤ P(E_1 or E_2 | both pass). Nothing more is identified.
  - **Where A2 fails.** The mask and its 2% false-alarm rate come from scripted round trips, which follow one route. Agents search, open other apps, grant permissions, leave drafts, change recents, keyboard history and clipboard, and run longer, so background writers (sync, media scanner, usage statistics) have more time. None of that is in a scripted mask. Each such residue is a "certificate" of nothing. Showing a false-alarm rate ≤ 2% at 95% confidence needs zero alarms in about 150 clean runs, and they must be clean agent or human runs matched for duration and apps opened.
  - **Factor 2.** P(E_1 or E_2) ≤ P(E_1) + P(E_2), so the mean per-leg rate among both-pass compositions is only bounded below by θ/2 unless annotators attribute each violation to a leg, at which point the bound is no longer label-free.
  - **Conditioning on leg 2 passing has no fixed sign.** P(E_1 | both pass) = P(E_1 | leg 1 passes) × λ, where λ compares how often leg 2 passes after a deviating leg 1 with how often it passes overall. If a wrong first leg makes the inverse infeasible, λ < 1 and the bound is conservative. If the inverse becomes vacuous ("delete X" when X was never created passes trivially, the pre-satisfied-goal effect the card files under R4), λ > 1 and the composed rate overstates the single-task rate. In that case the claimed lower bound on single-task passes is false.
  - **Leg 2 is a different task.** It starts from an agent-made state, with different distractor rows and a re-parameterised checker; its error rate is not that of the shipped delete task.
  - **Selection of reversible pairs.** No extrapolation from the applicable set to the suite is identified. The suite-level bound is (share of passes in the applicable set) × θ / 2 for per-pass rates; with a 15–20% share, θ = 3% gives 0.2–0.3% and θ = 10% gives 0.75–1%.
  - **What a round trip cannot see.** (i) A wrong requested effect that the inverse then removes (near-miss value added, then deleted) returns the device to start: two wrong legs, zero violation. (ii) Compensating errors. (iii) Transient effects done and undone. (iv) Anything outside the observer (messages sent, network traffic) or inside a masked field; the wider the mask, the looser the bound. (v) Same-table collateral rows on the SQLite pairs, because the shipped checker already fails those runs, so they never reach the both-pass set. The instrument is therefore a detector of residual out-of-table side effects and incomplete undo. Because its recall is unknown and mode-dependent, a low violation rate is not evidence of a low false-pass rate; the pre-specified "negative result" does not mean what the card says.
  - **Unit of analysis.** Checkers are deterministic per template: arXiv 2610.09142 reports identical verdicts across variants in 107 of 109 template-by-edit groups, and arXiv 2605.10448 states that its unit of uncertainty is the task, not the record. With 10–15 pairs, a cluster bootstrap has a lower limit of zero unless violations occur in at least 4 pairs (with 3 of 15, 3.5% of resamples contain none). The paper must declare whether templates are fixed (a statement about this suite) or sampled (then n is the number of pairs).
  - **Is the two-sided Rogan–Gladen step coherent?** Not as stated, for four reasons.
    1. A deterministic checker has no sensitivity of its own; "sensitivity" in a cell is the share of seeded instances the checker happens to reject, a property of the seed mix. At fine granularity every cell is 0 or 1. Cells at 1 contribute no false passes. Cells at 0 are where all false passes live, and there the checker records no failures, so the count to scale is zero and the estimate is 0/0. The estimator returns a number only in cells coarse enough to mix detectable and undetectable sub-modes, and that number is right only if the agent's mix equals the seeded mix. Stratification cannot remove this, because the informative cells are mixtures by construction.
    2. Specificity from scripted gold runs is close to 1 by construction (benchmark authors validated their checkers on their own reference path) and says nothing about correct agent runs that end in a different valid state. The correction is one-sided in practice. A real specificity needs audited FAIL verdicts, which the audit in step 6 does not sample.
    3. The estimator needs each failed run assigned to one failure mode. That is a labelling step with its own error, and "collateral change" is not exclusive of the others.
    4. The correction assumes checker error is the same for every agent within a mode; C2 claims checker error differs across agents. If agents differ within modes, C4 should fail; if only across modes, C4 can pass. Stating this turns the tension into a useful test (between-mode versus within-mode differential error).
    - Consequence: C4 success would not license correction for a new agent, and C4 failure would show only that this seed set does not match these agents.
  - **Is the blind audit a valid arbiter, and how large must it be?**
    - Validity. If annotators read the typed diff from the state observer, the arbiter shares the instrument's blind spots; if they read only screenshots, they miss off-screen effects (in arXiv 2607.28367, withholding screenshots cut the checker errors its two LLM judges detected from 14 to 8 and from 10 to 6, and its two human groups agreed at κ = 0.59 on a stress set). Either way the arbiter's own recall must be measured with spike-ins. Two annotators from the author group are blind to the instruments but not to the hypothesis. The rubric for "wrong" (native goal versus frame condition, severity) must be fixed before any run is seen.
    - Sample size at a true rate of 3%, independent passes, exact 95% intervals: n = 100 gives 0.6–8.5%; 300 gives 1.4–5.6%; 500 gives 1.7–4.9%; 1,000 gives 2.0–4.3%; 2,000 gives 2.3–3.8%. A half-width of one point needs about 1,000.
    - C4 accepts by default at small n. If the truth is 3% and the prediction is 6%, the prediction still falls inside the audit interval with probability 0.56 at n = 200, 0.41 at 300, 0.12 at 500, and 0.003 at 1,000; a prediction of 1.5% is accepted with probability 0.75 at 200 and 0.11 at 1,000. C4 needs an equivalence margin and roughly 500–1,000 independent audited passes per agent.
    - AndroidWorld cannot supply that. Passes cluster by template; at template level, 3 or 4 affected templates of 116 give intervals of 0.5–7.4% and 1.0–8.6%. The honest options are a census per agent (an exact figure for that agent on that suite, no sampling inference) or pooling several benchmarks.
    - C1's own power: with independence, P(lower bound > 3%) is 0.54 at a true rate of 6% and n = 200 both-pass compositions, 0.89 at n = 500, and 0.88 at a true rate of 8% and n = 200.

- Security-referee view (safety-benchmark variant: threat model, what a missed harm means, ethics):
  - **No threat model is stated.** Three are possible and they want different metrics. (T0) A non-adversarial agent that errs: prevalence is meaningful. (T1) Adversarial content in the environment steers the agent: the attacker's goals are typically exfiltration or irreversible actions. (T2) A developer or an optimiser games the checker: the adversary selects the blind modes, so average-case prevalence is irrelevant and the existence result (kill matrix) is the right metric. C3's "existence is not prevalence" is a point in favour under T0 and against under T2.
  - **Harms are mostly irreversible, relations need reversibility.** Sending a message, paying, sharing or deleting has no inverse task. The harms a safety benchmark exists for fall outside R1's applicable set.
  - **End-state diffs see integrity, not confidentiality.** Reading sensitive data and disclosing it, or doing harm and undoing it, leaves no end-state diff. The safety variant needs an event trace (network egress, intents, clipboard, notifications, logcat). Without it the variant covers persistent on-device integrity harms only and must say so.
  - **"diff ⊆ gold diff" is a whitelist that needs a gold diff per task.** That is the hand-written expected-diff baseline and contradicts "no per-task expected diff". For refusal tasks the gold diff is empty, so benign exploration residue becomes an alarm and the mask problem (A2) is at its worst.
  - **What a missed harm means.** A false "safe" verdict is false assurance, with an asymmetric cost. Assurance needs an upper bound on missed harm or a coverage argument. This instrument gives a one-sided lower bound: it can discredit a safety checker but never certify one, and a small bound must not be reported as validation.
  - **Adaptive agents.** A published mask is a map of where changes are invisible. The paper should report how the bound behaves if an agent confines its changes to masked fields.
  - **Observer integrity.** A rooted emulator can change app behaviour, and snapshotting live databases can itself alter state (assumption A3). A no-op control (snapshot, wait, snapshot) is required.
  - **Ethics.** Blind modes of a safety checker are dual-use: they tell an optimiser how to look safe. Expect a demand for coordinated disclosure to maintainers before any preprint, patched checkers released with the paper, and exploit-ready seeds for unpatched safety checks withheld; arXiv 2610.09142 and 2605.10448 both report contacting maintainers, so reviewers will treat that as the norm. Also needed: emulator and synthetic data only, API terms checked for harmful-task prompts, annotator exposure and independence described, benchmark licences stated. The ICML contribution type, as summarised, asks for licensing, provenance and annotation practice.

- Journal-referee view (JAIR/AIJ):
  - A journal version cannot disclaim the theory. Expected: formal definitions (verdict, postcondition, frame condition, relation soundness), a proposition giving the identified set for the per-pass error rate with sharpness, the selection factor λ treated explicitly, and the mode-misclassification analysis the card postpones. A cluster-robust inference section is mandatory.
  - Generality: at least three benchmark families (mobile, desktop or web, API-level stateful), with the applicability rule reported per suite. Automatic synthesis of inverse tasks is the most journal-worthy extension because it attacks the coverage problem; it needs its own validation (is a synthesised inverse exact?).
  - Complete threats-to-validity section: observer coverage, mask validity under agent routes, annotator reliability with spike-ins, and results that persist across benchmark versions.
  - Fit: AIJ may judge the AI content thin and suggest a software-engineering journal (TOSEM, EMSE); JAIR is more open to evaluation methodology. Neither journal's scope page was opened this session, and the venue profile lists quartiles as unverified. The safety material is better as a separate, properly threat-modelled study than as a section.
  - Checker repair from surviving faults is routine unless fixes are accepted upstream and the re-scored leaderboard is published.

- What would make this a clear accept:
  1. **Change the design so the estimand is a property of ordinary benchmark passes (the single most important change).** Run the agent on the shipped task from the shipped initial state; then apply a *scripted* inverse (or compare with the scripted reference end state). A non-empty masked diff then implies that this one standard run deviated: no second verdict to condition on, no factor 2, no re-parameterised checker, and the population is the leaderboard's own. Keep agent-plus-agent round trips, idempotence and commutation as secondary instruments for tasks with several valid end states, where the gold differential is unsound.
  2. State the result as a proposition: estimand, assumptions A1–A3, the identified set, and what each violated assumption does to the bound. Separate "goal not achieved" from "side effect", with severity.
  3. Validate the mask on adjudicated-clean agent and human runs, report the full trade-off between false alarms and recall on planted writes, and make C1's threshold net of the measured false-alarm rate.
  4. Make the audit a census on each small suite, and elsewhere a two-phase design that uses instrument flags to stratify. Under illustrative values (3% true rate, flag precision 0.8, flag recall 0.6) optimal allocation needs about half the labels of simple random sampling, and about one sixth at recall 0.9. That efficiency result is a statistical contribution ICML can credit.
  5. Three benchmark families, including one API-level stateful suite where runs are cheap and inverses are common, so n is in the thousands and templates in the dozens.
  6. Effects that matter: non-benign violations in a material share of passes spread over many templates, rank changes that survive seed resampling, fixes accepted by maintainers.
  7. Demote seeding to a short section framed as the between-mode versus within-mode test, with an equivalence margin, or drop it.
  8. Release the observer, relations, masks, per-run diffs, audit labels and patched checkers.
